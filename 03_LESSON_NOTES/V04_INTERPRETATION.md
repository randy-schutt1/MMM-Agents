## 1. WORKING UNDERSTANDING

**Confidence for this summary as a whole: MEDIUM-HIGH for Segment A, LOW for treating
Segment B as method.**

V04 is the course's first genuinely **prescriptive** lesson. V01 framed the weekly cycle,
V02 and V03 taught anatomy and scaling; V04 finally says *when you are and are not allowed
to press the button*, and says it as a short list.

The argument has one shape, and it is a **filter on location, not on pattern**:

1. The pattern (M/W) is not the edge. The same M that lost money inside the Asian range
   would have made money 25–50 pips outside it — that is the GBP/JPY story at
   `[00:03:04]`–`[00:03:34]`, and it is the whole lesson in one anecdote.
2. Therefore the tradeable event is not "an M appeared" but "**the dealer hit the stops,
   and *then* an M appeared**".
3. The stop hunt is what converts the pattern from noise into signal, because it is the
   moment the dealer has taken the liquidity he needed and must now correct against the
   positions he induced (`[00:12:30]`–`[00:12:48]`).

Everything else in Segment A serves that: the 25–50 pip distance measures "far enough
outside to be a stop hunt"; the second leg confirms the hunt failed; TDI confirms
exhaustion; the tight stop is affordable *because* the entry is at the extreme.

**What makes V04 unusually good evidence:** the criteria are delivered as a self-contained
list the instructor himself labels *"the criteria"*, twice, and calls *"Simple"*. This is
not an agent assembling a procedure from scattered remarks (the risk flagged in V03's
generalization audit §3.3) — the assembly is the instructor's.

**What still stops it short of codable:** condition C3 requires TDI, and TDI is deferred to
"next week" for the second lesson running. V04 states a rule it does not equip you to
apply.

## 3. GENERALIZATION AUDIT

Statements above that widen the source, checked deliberately. (V01's `E02` — unsupported
generalization — is this project's second-most-common defect, and V03 R1 charged one.)

1. **I1 calls the three conditions a conjunction.** The instructor lists them without
   connectives: *"25 to 50 pips above and below the blue box. M formation second leg, W
   formation second leg, TDI confirms…"*. Read as a disjunction the rule would permit
   entering on TDI alone anywhere on the chart, which contradicts §2b's prohibition; the
   conjunctive reading is the only one consistent with the rest of the lesson. **Still a
   reading.** Logged as Q1.
2. **I1(a) says "25–50 pips beyond the box" is a *condition*.** Three of the four
   utterances are **descriptive** — *"The dealer vectors 25 to 50 pips above…"* `[00:10:01]`,
   *"You have 25 to 50 pips higher than the Asian range"* `[00:14:36]` — describing what the
   dealer does, not instructing the student. Only `[00:15:43]`, inside the criteria list,
   is prescriptive. **The prescriptive reading rests on one utterance.** Logged as Q2.
3. **I1 treats 25–50 as a band with a floor and a ceiling.** Whether >50 pips disqualifies
   a setup is **never stated**. The instructor only ever says the dealer goes 25–50; he
   never says "and not more". Reading the upper bound as a filter would be an invention.
   **I1 therefore asserts the floor and is silent on the ceiling.** Logged as Q3.
4. **I3's "second leg close" appears exactly once**, at `[00:08:56]`, inside a sentence
   whose main clause is the "15 to 45 minutes. Guaranteed" claim. Elsewhere the entry is
   located loosely — *"somewhere in here"* `[00:08:42]`, *"Anyone of these bars?"*
   `[00:08:01]`. The precise reading may be over-reading a stray phrase. Graded
   Medium-High rather than High for that reason.
5. **I6's "same event at two scales"** is stronger than the source. The instructor says
   they are *coordinated* and form *at the same time*; "the same event" is my compression.
   A weaker and equally supported reading is "two reliably co-occurring events". Logged.
6. **I5 is deliberately NOT widened past the four-hour chart.** The instructor's sentence
   is scoped to *"on the four hour"*, twice. Whether an M on any timeframe is an anchor
   point is not said. See §5.
7. **Source notes §5's 11-step sequence** is assembled from passages up to 20 minutes
   apart. The instructor teaches the pieces and states the criteria, but never numbers
   them as one procedure. **That assembly is interpretation**, and it lives in the source
   notes' §5 flagged as "SEQUENCE / PROCEDURE TAUGHT" — the same soft spot V03's audit
   flagged. A reviewer should treat the *ordering* as mine and the *contents* as his.

## 5. `I7` FROM V01 — THE ANCHOR-POINT QUESTION, ADVANCED

**`REVIEW_INDEX.md` open research item 2** has been carried since V01 R1: *"whether
'anchor point', 'peak formation high/low' and 'M or W formation' are one concept. Stays
`INFERRED / Low`; **re-adjudicate at V02**."* It was not adjudicated at V02 or V03.

**V04 supplies the first direct statement.** Answering a student by name:

> *"No, Alex, if you see an AM on the four hour that's your anchor point, if you got an AM
> on the four hour charge that's your peak formation high, then you're looking for stop on
> high drop, stop on high drop W get out."* `[00:21:55]`–`[00:22:02]`

Reading (`EXPLICIT` / **Medium-High**, not High):

- The two clauses are **parallel and predicate the same subject**. The same antecedent —
  an M on the four-hour chart — is called an *anchor point* in the first and a *peak
  formation high* in the second. On the plainest reading the instructor is naming one
  object twice.
- This makes **all three of V01's terms one concept**, at least on the four-hour chart:
  the M formation *is* the anchor point *is* the peak formation high.
- The consequent is the trade: having found it, *"then you're looking for stop on high drop"* (ASR: stop-hunt high, drop).
  That is coherent with §2c — the anchor is the reference the stop hunt is measured against.

**Why Medium-High and not High, stated plainly:**

1. **The passage is ASR-damaged.** *"an AM"* for "an M" and *"charge"* for "chart" are
   repairs. They are safe repairs — "AM" appears nowhere else as a term, and the same
   substitution recurs at `[00:21:53]` — but they are repairs. **The two load-bearing
   terms, "anchor point" and "peak formation high", are rendered cleanly**, which is why
   this is not graded lower.
2. **It is one sentence, in Q&A, spoken quickly.** It is not a slide, not repeated, and not
   the subject of the segment.
3. **It is scoped to the four-hour chart.** Whether the identity holds at other scales is
   not stated, and V01's usage was not four-hour-specific. **The identity is therefore
   asserted only where the instructor asserted it.**
4. **It says nothing about the low side.** "Peak formation *low*" and W are not mentioned
   in this sentence. Symmetry is likely and is **not** assumed.

**Recommended disposition:** open item 2 moves from `INFERRED / Low` to `EXPLICIT /
Medium-High, four-hour scale only`. It is **not** closed — closing it wants either a slide
or a second independent statement, ideally one covering the W/low side. `A-010` (peak
formation high or low) and `A-011` (M and W formation) are both extended with this
evidence rather than resolved.
