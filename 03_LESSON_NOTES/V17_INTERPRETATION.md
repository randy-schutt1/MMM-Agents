## §1 — THE LESSON'S CLAIM, RESTATED IN MY OWN WORDS

**V17 argues that a trend is a three-day inventory cycle run by the dealer, and that everything
retail calls "trend" is the visible surface of that cycle.**

Day 1 the dealer reverses at a peak formation and it surprises everyone, because every conventional
indicator is still pointing the other way. Day 2 the conventional indicators catch up and fire —
50/200 crossover, MACD zero line, CCI, RSI 50 — which is precisely when retail enters. Day 3 the
dealer accelerates, fans the moving averages, prints three vector candles at the end of the third
leg, and everyone who was waiting for confirmation piles in at the top. **He has now sold to them
for three days and is heavy net short; the only way he books that inventory is to reverse against
them.** So the acceleration is not the trend's strength, it is the trend's obituary.

**Two things sit on top of that skeleton.** First, a *reset* — the same shape as a reversal, at the
same landmarks (the Mayo and the Blueberry, where retail orders pile up), but a continuation: it
re-anchors the peak formation and buys three more days. Second, the *safety trade*, which he spends
the last three minutes insisting should be every student's signature trade, because it does not
require you to guess the high or low of the week — **the dealer has already told you where it is by
stop-hunting it.**

**The first half of the lesson is the audit** — where are you, what should you be able to do by
week 8, here are two graded quizzes to find out. And the answer to the second quiz is the most
completely specified setup checklist in the corpus.

## §5 — Q3: THE THREE-DAY CYCLE — CAN IT BE TESTED, AND WHAT DID I CHOOSE TO TEST?

**The cycle as stated is not directly testable, and I want to be plain about why before saying what
I did test.**

*"Day 1 is a reversal day"* requires identifying a peak formation, which requires `A-011`/`A-010`,
which are open. *"Day 3 shows acceleration"* requires *"angle and separation"*, undefined. *"Most of
the time"* is the reliability claim and has no number. **A backtest of the cycle would be a backtest
of my reconstruction of it, and that is `D-027`'s trap.**

**So `PT-045` tests two things the lesson states without any undefined term in them:**

1. ⭐ **The wick claim** (`item 11`): *"the wick on the daily candle represents the consolidation off
   of the high or off of the low."* Falsifiable as stated: if daily wicks are consolidation
   remnants, the **closing hour** of the FX day should sit inside the wick's price band far more
   often than chance. **No pattern recognition required.**
2. ⭐ **The 3-day unidirectional swing** (`item 1`), tested in its weakest and most generous form:
   after a daily directional reversal, does price continue in the new direction for **2–4 days**
   more often than a matched null? **Reversal is defined mechanically** (a daily close that reverses
   the prior 3-day sign), which is *not* the lesson's peak formation and is declared as a proxy.

**What I deliberately did NOT test:** the safety trade (needs `V`/`W`), the shark-fin 50-pip claim
(needs `A-084`), the `333` (needs *"vector candle"*), the pivot-zone shift (needs three undefined
antecedents). **Four of the lesson's five most interesting claims are untestable by this project
today, and that is a finding about the corpus rather than about V17.**
