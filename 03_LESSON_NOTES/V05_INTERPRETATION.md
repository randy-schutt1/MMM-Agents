## 1. WORKING UNDERSTANDING

**Confidence for this summary as a whole: HIGH as a reading of what the lesson is,
NOT APPLICABLE as a contribution to method.**

V05 is a **methods-of-study lesson**. Its subject is the *process* by which a student
converts screen time into recognition — mark the chart, label it, save it, cut it into
flashcards, repeat — and not the content of any setup. `EXPLICIT`, from the lesson's own
framing at `[00:10:07]`–`[00:10:20]` and its printed task list.

The argument has one shape, and the presenter states it in his first ninety seconds:

1. The problem is not *knowing* the pattern; it is **seeing it at the hard right edge**,
   where the future half of the chart is missing (`[00:01:29]`, and the idea recurs at
   `[00:18:39]`, `[00:19:54]`).
2. Recognition at the right edge is a **trained perceptual skill**, not a deduction — hence
   Arbo's six months of marking charts without trading (`[00:02:16]`) and the flashcards.
3. Therefore the correct daily activity is **rehearsal against known outcomes**: mark up
   history where the answer is visible, so the shape is familiar when the answer is not.
4. The corollary is a **pass discipline** — if the rehearsed shape is not present, there is
   no trade (`[00:25:05]`, `[00:28:09]`, and seven more).

`INFERRED`, but only lightly: steps 1–4 are each stated; the claim that they form one
argument is mine.

**Why the pass discipline dominates.** Counting the source notes' §5d, the presenter
declines a trade or instructs the student to decline one at **nine distinct points**, more
often than he describes any entry. My reading is that this is the lesson's actual
behavioural payload, and that it follows necessarily from (2): if recognition is trained,
then *not yet recognising* is the expected state most of the time, and acting anyway is the
characteristic error. `INFERRED`. **Confidence: MEDIUM-HIGH** — the count is objective, the
causal story is mine.

## 2. WHAT V05 CONTRIBUTES, GIVEN THAT IT CONTRIBUTES NO RULES

Ranked by what I judge their actual value to the project to be.

### 2.1 A specification of the study process itself — the real deliverable `IMPLIED`

The course has, until now, said *do your homework* without saying what homework is. V05
answers that: find the structure, box it, label it, write the days, save the image, cut the
flashcard at the decision candle, review weekly with someone. **This is the first
operational description of the study loop in the corpus**, and — crucially — it is
*procedure*, which D-025 admits.

**This is the part of V05 the project should actually adopt**, and it is what
`V05_HOMEWORK.md` executes. **Confidence: HIGH.**

### 2.2 Independent evidence that the vocabulary means what the project thinks `VISUAL`

A practitioner who learned the method directly, speaking without the instructor present,
uses *anchor*, *level*, *second leg*, *induce*, *trap*, *railroad tracks*, *three hits to
the high*, *hard right edge* fluently and consistently with V01–V04. The slides print
*"1/2 Batman"* — the corpus's first written form of a term the instructor speaks
(V02 `[00:20:37]`) — and *"3 Hits to the Hi or Lo"*.

**Why this matters more than it looks.** The project's terminology has so far rested almost
entirely on one man's speech through an imperfect ASR. A second independent speaker using
the same terms the same way is real corroboration that the glossary is not an artifact of
transcription. `INFERRED`. **Confidence: MEDIUM-HIGH.**

### 2.3 A map of how much of the method is NOT in this library `INFERRED`

**This is the most consequential thing V05 tells the project, and it is bad news.**

The presenter defers the operative detail to the **DMR** — a separate programme with its own
video library — at nine points, and specifically for: **levels and reset** (`[00:10:51]`),
**what makes a pattern nameable and how big railroad tracks must be** (`[00:26:45]`–`[00:26:51]`),
**traps** (`[00:56:48]`), and **signature trades and checklists** (`[00:33:17]`).

Three of those are questions the bootcamp videos have left open across five lessons. The
reading I draw: **the 21 bootcamp videos are not a self-contained specification of the
method, and were never meant to be.** They are the lecture half of a programme whose
worked-detail half lived elsewhere.

If that is right, then some open `A-xxx` records may be **unresolvable from this corpus in
principle** rather than merely unresolved so far, and the project should eventually
distinguish the two. **Confidence: MEDIUM.** It rests on one guest's repeated deferrals; a
later lesson could show the bootcamp covering this material itself. → **`A-042`**, and
flagged for `REVIEW_INDEX.md`.

### 2.4 The best idea in the lesson, recorded and excluded `EXPLICIT`

The stop-hunt / trap-move discriminator (source notes §5h): **close beyond the level versus
pin beyond without closing**, plus **immediate directional shift versus oscillation**.

This is testable, falsifiable, and distinguishes two objects the instructor uses constantly
and has never separated on the record. It is also **guest, and therefore excluded**. My
reading of its status: a **research question of unusually high priority** — worth putting to
the remaining 16 lessons as *"does the instructor ever draw this distinction himself?"* — and
nothing more until one of them does. → **`A-049`**. **Confidence: HIGH** that the
discriminator is what he said; **NOT APPLICABLE** as method.
