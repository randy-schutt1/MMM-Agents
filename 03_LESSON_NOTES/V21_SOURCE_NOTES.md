### The worked example — `[00:21:26]`–`[00:24:05]`

Three-lot trader, a 0–100 grid, 50 the midpoint. **⚠️ `[00:22:10]` declares the numbers are for
arithmetic convenience:** *"I'm not telling you [to use] the hundred-pip stop […] I'm using these
numbers to make the math simple."*

| Structure | Loss |
|---|---|
| **3 lots at entry**, stopped 100 away | `3 × 100` = **−300** |
| ⭐ **1 lot at entry + 2 lots at half the distance**, same stop | `1 × 100` + `2 × 50` = **−200** |

> `[00:24:02]` *"**You essentially lost at a discount** — but you gave yourself another opportunity
> to buy or sell the stop-loss level for a chance to get square."*

⭐ `[00:29:xx]`–`[00:30:11]` *"the only way to do it is to **aggregate your risk** […] taking 1 %,
1 % and 1 % […] gives yourself a chance to **beat the dealer instead of the dealer beating you by
triggering your stops**."*

⚠️ **`[00:35:20]` states the mechanism the whole idea rests on:** *"the dealer **has no way to
determine that you are holding a pending order** at the next level stop trigger. He doesn't know
what's up there. **He just sees money up there.**"* ⛔ **This is an assertion about dealer
information and the lesson offers no evidence for it.**

> Upon entry *"you think you see an M"*: **a market order opened, with TWO pending orders, 20 pips

linebackers."*
