## 1. WORKING UNDERSTANDING — WHAT THIS LESSON IS ABOUT

`EXPLICIT`, and unusually easy to state, because the deck states it itself.

**V07 is an argument that "a good trade" is not the same thing as "a big trade", and that the
difference is decided before the trade rather than after it.**

The presenter opens with a gallery of winning charts, immediately asks whether they *"tell the
whole story"* `[00:00:32]`, and answers no — twice, with his own losing examples
(`[00:01:06]`, `[00:02:38]`). He then enumerates four candidate definitions of a good trade
(setups/levels, pattern, exit, risk-to-reward), **declines to rank them** `[00:04:38]`, gives his
own preference as an opinion, and closes with a synthesis: the best trades are the ones you have
already seen many times in your own research, recognised while forming, and taken at a location
you were waiting for.

The operative content, as I read it, is a **three-part loop**:

```text
R&D  ──►  FLASHCARD  ──►  PATIENCE
 │          (a screenshot of the exact       (wait for that
 │           setup you will take)             exact thing)
 └── count how many times it occurred over a year ◄──┘
```

`[00:12:09]` is the loop's measurable step — *"How many times over a year is what I am looking
for actually occurred"* — and it is the only assignment in the lesson that a present-day agent
can perform.

## 2. WHAT V07 CONTRIBUTES TO THE CORPUS

### 2.1 The base-rate objection, stated from inside the course `EXPLICIT`

This is the contribution I rate highest, and it is not a trading rule.

> `[00:06:32]` *"The pattern is what we look at and M's and W's are everywhere."*
> `[00:06:39]` *"How do we differentiate which ones work and which ones don't?"*
> `[00:12:14]`–`[00:12:21]` *"If you go back and look for just patterns, you will see M's and W's
> everywhere. They are going to be all over the place everywhere in the day in different ranges."*

**A speaker with full normative authority under `D-033` says that the corpus's central pattern
object is ubiquitous, and that ubiquity is a problem.** Every earlier lesson treats the M/W as a
signal; this one says the raw signal has an unknown and probably high base rate, and that the
information must therefore live in the *filter* (level, location, confirmation), not in the shape.

That is precisely the argument `D-026` encodes as project method — a hit rate without a
comparator is unreadable. **Recording it here matters because it means the project's baseline
requirement is not an external imposition on the course; the course makes the same objection to
itself.** `08_CONCEPT_LIBRARY/` gains nothing codable from it, and the corpus gains a citation it
did not have.

### 2.2 The level → expected-pattern mapping `EXPLICIT`, and it is new

Earlier lessons use *level* as a position counter. V07 is the first to attach an **expectation**
to each level and to make that expectation a **reason not to trade**:

| Level | Expect | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | *"an A pattern or a single leg"*; **not** a second leg | `[00:14:07]`–`[00:14:15]` |
| 1 | If a second leg appears anyway, *"that's a gift"* — take it | `[00:14:16]`, `[00:25:04]` |
| 1 | *"from that reset, you're not expecting a W. If they give it to you, it's a bonus"* | `[00:39:37]`–`[00:39:44]` |
| 1 / 2 | Tradable **in the direction of bias only**, if the pattern is clean | `[00:32:44]`–`[00:32:52]` |
| 3 | Where the trap is set, *"probably not with the same amount of money"* elsewhere | `[00:29:02]`–`[00:29:29]` |
| 3 | Where a second leg is the expected object, and where he waits | `[00:05:00]`, `[00:34:55]` |

**The discipline this produces is stated as a prohibition** `[00:27:47]`–`[00:28:00]`: a valid
*looking* W at level two is passed, because he is waiting for level three.

> **`D-030` blocks coding this and I am not going to pretend otherwise.** *Level* is `A-004` and
> undefined; *A pattern* is undefined (`A-057`, opened by this lesson); *second leg* is `A-007`.
> What survives as a **structural** contribution is the shape of the rule — **the course filters
> patterns by location, and treats an off-location pattern as a reason to wait rather than a
> weaker signal.** That shape is citable; the thresholds are not.

### 2.3 A four-way second-leg taxonomy `EXPLICIT`, and the first time the corpus has one

`[00:29:56]`–`[00:31:03]` enumerates four second-leg geometries and says which two he trades:

| Variant | Geometry | Traded? |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Induces **below** the first leg's low, then railroad-tracks back into range | ✅ *"on the next very next candle"* |
| 2 | *"tilted"* / *"slanted"* away | ❌ *"I haven't done enough homework on them"* |
| 3 | Exactly **at** the same level | ❌ (not separately endorsed) |
| 4 | Exactly at the level, **pins through**, comes back and gives a **confirmed candle** | ✅ |

`A-044` asks *what makes a second leg a "nameable pattern"*. V06 answered with **candle names**;
V07 answers with **geometries**. **These are different axes and I have deliberately not merged
them** — a session that combined V06's three candle patterns with V07's four geometries would
manufacture a twelve-cell rule table neither speaker stated, which is `REVIEW_PROTOCOL.md` §17
failure mode 3 in its purest form. `A-044` is **extended twice and closed neither time.**

### 2.4 "Hi-Lo" — a named primary method the course keeps deferring `EXPLICIT`

`[00:07:13]` calls entry at the day's extreme the potential *"holy grail of daily trading"*, the
closing synopsis slide recommends it in print (*"Couple it with a Hi-Lo entry for smaller risk and
maximum gain"*), and the chart panel prints `Pips To HOD` / `Pips To LOD` live. **And the lesson
does not teach it.** It is credited to *"Jim"* three times (`[00:07:38]`, `[00:21:16]`,
`[00:38:26]`) and deferred to a future presentation.

This is the `A-039` shape — a component treated as load-bearing and never defined — and it is
logged as **`A-056`**. But it differs from `A-039` in a way that matters:

> **TDI cannot be tested because nobody has said what it is. Hi-Lo cannot be *traded* because
> nobody has said how to find the extreme in real time — but its *ceiling* can be measured
> exactly, because "high of the day" and "low of the day" are arithmetic once a day boundary is
> fixed.** That asymmetry is what `06_MANUAL_BACKTEST/V07/BT_V07_0001.md` exploits, and it is the
> only claim in V07 I could find that is testable without approximating a single undefined term.

## 5. TENSIONS AND WHAT I DID NOT RESOLVE

### 5.1 The lesson recommends a method it does not teach `UNRESOLVED`

The closing synopsis — the deck's own summary of itself — makes Hi-Lo half of the
recommendation, and the lesson contains no Hi-Lo instruction at all. I cannot tell whether this
is (a) a pointer to a lesson elsewhere in the DMR (`A-042`), (b) an assumption that the audience
already knows it from the author's own material, or (c) genuinely unresolved inside the
programme. **I did not pick one.**

### 5.2 The four questions are never answered `EXPLICIT`, and I think that is deliberate

The deck asks *"Do they matter?"*, *"Do they help?"*, *"Is it really important?"*, *"Isn't that
all that matters?"* and answers none of them with a rule. `[00:04:38]` says all four are valid.
`[00:10:14]` asks whether a middle ground exists and `[00:10:29]` answers *"Yes, I believe so"* —
which is the whole resolution.

**A session under pressure to extract doctrine could read this lesson as stating four rules.** It
states four *considerations* and one preference. I am flagging this because it is the most likely
way for a future reader to over-extract from V07.

### 5.3 "You may only trade 50% of what you see" `UNRESOLVED`

`[00:31:29]`. I cannot tell whether this is (a) a descriptive estimate of his own selectivity,
(b) an instruction, or (c) a reassurance to a student worried about missing setups. The
surrounding sentences support (c) most strongly, and the number is precise enough to be mistaken
for (b). **Recorded as a number, not adopted as a filter rate** (`D-010`).

### 5.4 The drawdown claim is the most testable thing he says, and I could not test it `EXPLICIT`

`[00:08:04]` *"Second legs confirm[ed] have little to no drawdown"* — a falsifiable,
consequential claim about a specific setup, hedged immediately (`[00:08:09]` *"I won't say all of
the time"*). **`D-030` blocks it**: *second leg* is `A-007` and undefined, so any test would
measure my definition and attribute the result to him. This is the fourth lesson in a row to
produce backtest debt of exactly this kind, and that accumulation is the correct behaviour, not a
backlog to clear by lowering the bar.

### 5.5 One thing I noticed and cannot explain `UNRESOLVED`

**GBP/USD does not appear in this lesson at all** — not as an example, not in the DMR-10 mention,
not in the *"all the dollar pairs"* remark at `[00:31:40]`. The examples are AUD/USD, EUR/USD and
EUR/JPY. Across V01–V07 the project's declared primary instrument (`D-007`) has been strikingly
under-represented in the course's own examples. I do not know whether that is coincidence,
a property of what happened to set up in March 2012, or something about the pair. **Recorded as
an observation, not a finding.**

## 7. SUMMARY FOR THE MASTERY REPORT

pattern is ubiquitous and therefore uninformative without a location filter; the first
level-by-level table of *what to expect*, used as a reason to pass trades rather than take them;

the ADR lookback, not of a moving-average period. Six lessons in, `A-020`'s Required Research
route has now failed six times, and V07 failed it while having both halves of the answer on
screen at once.

**Dimension B (Recognition) is expected to be blocked**, for the same reason V06's was: the
objects to be recognised are `D-030`-blocked. **Dimension G is expected to be satisfiable**, and
narrowly — through the one claim in the lesson that survives `D-030`.
