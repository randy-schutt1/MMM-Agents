## 5. THE ALLOCATION CHANGE — WHY THE OLD PROCESS WON'T SCALE

The V01–V21 pipeline treated each video as **a lesson to be mastered**: full transcript → source
notes → interpretation notes → mastery report → gated reviewer round → ledger updates. It
produced excellent evidence discipline. It also produced **141 ambiguities and 31 contradictions
across 21 videos**, took months, and is sequentially gated.

Applying it unchanged to 30 more hours would predictably yield another ~200 open ambiguities and
would not converge. The problem has also changed shape:

> **Before: "no source states this" — an acquisition problem.
> Now: "several sources state it and disagree" — an adjudication and testing problem.**

So the allocation should shift from *ingest-then-discover* to **query-driven retrieval against a
fixed question list**, with the expensive lesson-artifact treatment reserved for passages that
actually answer a question.

   need no Ruffle pipeline, and frames extract directly with `ffmpeg -ss`. This removes the

## 6. THREE CHEAP TESTS THAT COULD CLOSE GAPS QUICKLY

### 6.1 Blue tracer — the candidate set is already narrower than it looks

Pt 2 tempts an easy answer (800 EMA = "blueberry" = the dark blue line). **The V19 evidence argues
against it.** `04_SCREENSHOTS/V19/INDEX.md` §2(h) records that the frames carry exactly **two**
blue-family candidates — a **cyan dashed horizontal level** and a **cyan solid sloping average** —
and that no frame prints a period beside a colour. Two further constraints:

- Under the BTMM colour convention the **50 EMA is "water" (aqua/cyan)**, so the cyan *sloping*
  line in V19 is most likely the 50 — a line Mauro already has another name for.
- The `06:45` checklist frame reads `Use ADR and Hi/Lo Markers`, and the mangled ASR of that exact
  line is *"I low market the blue tracer"* (INDEX §2(e)). That ties the phrase to a **marker**, not
  a moving average — and it agrees with the (unverified, closed-source) MQL5 BTMM product claim
  that the blue tracer draws the **prior day's high/low**.
- No dark-blue slow average is reported in the V19 frames at all, which is what an 800 EMA would
  look like.

**Therefore the discriminating test is cheap and does not need new material:** identify the
instrument and date on a V19 chart frame, reconstruct the series, and check (a) whether the cyan
sloping line matches a 50 EMA, and (b) whether the cyan dashed horizontal sits at the prior day's
high or low. If both hold, `A-133` resolves to a Hi/Lo marker on evidence rather than assertion —
and the 800-EMA reading is eliminated. Run this **before** letting any Tier-6 colour convention
suggest an answer. **Highest value-per-hour item in the program.**

Other cheap tests:
1. **Level-break segmentation.** Code BEEKAY's rule (a new level requires breaking the prior
   swing high/low) on the development block and check whether two independent reviewers marking
   the same charts by hand agree with it. That is precisely the completion evidence the gap
   matrix asks for on `A-004`/`A-007`.
2. **The 8-candle PSR.** Compute the first 8 H1 candles of each week, then measure how often the
   subsequent stop hunt marks the week's extreme. This directly attacks `A-010` — the #1 blocker —
   and is fully prospective, so it cannot be accused of lookahead.

## 7. ON "NO MORE GAPS" AND ON PROFITABILITY — THE HONEST POSITION

**These are two different programs and conflating them is the main risk ahead.**

Closing every definitional gap gets you a *specifiable* method. It does not get you a *profitable*
one. The evidence already in this repository argues for caution: of the course's testable numeric
claims, **five are contradicted by GBP/USD data** — the week's extremes print on Friday, not
Tuesday/Wednesday (`PT-028`); last week's peak is breached about half the time despite being
taught as absolute (`PT-030`); a new session continues rather than reverses the prior one
(`PT-006`); the loss-streak claim is false at the course's own hit rate (`PT-035`); and both of
V10's quantitative claims fail, one against its own specificity control (`PT-036`). The recurring
pattern the cumulative summary identifies is: *the direction and the distance are frequently real;
the specific numeric premise almost never holds as stated.*

So the realistic target is not "the gaps close and it is profitable." It is:

> **Make the method specified enough that a fair test can be run — then run that test once,
> properly, and believe the result.**

Three things protect that:

1. **Pre-registration.** Now that rules are finally testable, the temptation is to tune thresholds
   until a backtest looks good. Every test states its hypothesis, metric, control, and decision
   rule *before* the runner exists. That discipline is why this repo's negatives are trustworthy.
2. **Costs.** No result counts as an edge until spread, slippage and swap are modeled. The
   development corpus has no spread in it.
3. **Spend the holdout once.** `2016-07-01 → 2017-12-29` has never been opened, and the D-044
   years are only partly contaminated (§4). That is your one clean shot at an honest answer. It
   should be fired at a complete, frozen rule set — entry, stop, size, management, exit, no-trade —
   and not before. A holdout opened on a half-specified system is simply gone.

**Shortest credible path:** Track A transcription → adjudicate the P0 primitives via Track B
(Mauro first, then test the Tier-6 candidates) → assemble one complete lifecycle at explicit
tiers → sealed practical → reconstruction retest → *then*, and only then, a costed
out-of-sample run.
