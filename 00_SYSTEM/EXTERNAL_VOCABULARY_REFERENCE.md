## 1. WHY THIS FILE EXISTS, AND WHY IT IS DANGEROUS

The owner asked for outside-the-course definitions of the terms the corpus names and never
defines, so that a human reading the register has *some* idea what the disputed words might
mean while the definitional debt keeps accruing.

That is a legitimate need and this file serves it. It is also the single most likely file in
this repository to cause the exact failure the project was built to prevent, because it puts
plausible, confident, well-sourced definitions of `push`, `the level`, `M/W` and `TDI` in
writing, inside the repo, in the same voice as everything else. A future session that reads
this file and forgets which side of the line it sits on will substitute an internet definition
for a course definition, run a backtest against it, and produce a number that gets attributed
to the instructor. That is `E06` + `E18`, and `D-030` was written specifically to stop it.

So the file carries three permanent structural defences:

1. **The banner above**, which is not to be softened, moved, or summarised away.
2. **Every entry names the record it relates to and restates that the record is unchanged.**
3. **Every entry carries the reconciliation instruction in §3** — this file *loses* to the
   course, always, whenever the course finally speaks.

### What a future session may and may not do with this file

| May | May NOT |
|---|---|
| Read it to orient a human who is confused by the vocabulary | Cite it as evidence in an `A-xxx`, `C-xxx`, mastery report, or review |
| Use it to write a **sharper Required Research question** ("does the course's ADR use 5 days?") | Use it to answer a Required Research question |
| Use it to notice that a course usage *conflicts* with the wider tradition, and log that as a finding about the course | Use it to fill a gap the course leaves, in notes, spec, Pine, or backtest |
| Compare a **future** in-course definition against it, per §3 | Let it stand as the definition because the course "probably means the same thing" |

## 2. LINEAGE FINDING — CORRECTS THE WORKING ASSUMPTION

The research brief assumed this course sits in the **ICT / Inner Circle Trader** tradition.
**That is probably wrong, and the correction matters more than any definition in this file.**

The corpus's own vocabulary — an instructor called **"Steve"**, a **three-level / three-day
cycle**, **M and W patterns** as the market maker's reversal footprint, **Asian box**,
**Brinks trade**, **Shadow Box**, **railroad tracks**, **TDI with a shark fin**, and moving
averages nicknamed after **condiments** — matches, item for item, the published vocabulary of
**Steve Mauro's "Beat The Market Maker" (BTMM)**, also marketed as *The Market Maker Method*.
ICT (Michael Huddleston) is an adjacent but **separate** lineage with its own terms (order
blocks, fair value gaps, the silver bullet) that appear **nowhere** in this corpus.

Practical consequences:

- Searches should target **BTMM / Steve Mauro**, not ICT. Definitions taken from ICT material
  would be a *different school's* vocabulary and would be worse than nothing.
- The course's own dating (2012 seminar recordings, `Bootcamp1 Wk2`) is consistent with the
  BTMM seminar era, and much of the public material below is derived from **student notes of
  those same seminars** — see the reliability warning in §4.
- **This is a hypothesis about provenance, not a proven identification.** No recording in this
  library states "BTMM" or "Steve Mauro" on screen as far as the corpus records. It should be
  logged as a research lead, not as a fact, and it is **not** grounds for importing anything.

## 4. HOW RELIABLE ARE THESE SOURCES? (READ BEFORE USING ANY OF IT)

Sourcing for this vocabulary is **structurally poor**, and honesty about that is part of the
deliverable:

- **No official, first-party BTMM glossary was found.** Steve Mauro's own paid course material
  is not publicly published; there is no equivalent of a canonical documentation site.
- The bulk of what exists is **third- and fourth-hand**: student seminar notes re-uploaded to
  document-sharing sites (Studocu, Course Hero, PDFCoffee, Scribd), broker/affiliate blog posts
  written for SEO, forum threads, and indicator product listings. Several of these are
  **derivatives of each other**, so agreement between two of them is often not independent
  corroboration — it can be the same note passed along twice.
- Document-sharing hosts are **paywalled or partially rendered**, so several results below were
  read through search-engine excerpts rather than the full document. Those are marked.
- The same term is used **differently by different sub-communities**, and where that happened it
  is recorded as competing candidates rather than silently resolved.

**Confidence scale used below:**

| Mark | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 🟢 **CONVERGENT** | Multiple independent-looking sources say the same thing; still external, still non-normative |
| 🟡 **CANDIDATES** | Two or more plausible readings exist externally; no basis to choose |
| 🔴 **UNCLEAR** | The wider tradition does not define it either, or sources are too thin/derivative to report a definition |

## 5. THE TERMS

### 5.1 — "push" · relates to `A-054`, `A-038`, and the `D-030` blocker on dimension B

**Course status: UNCHANGED — `DO NOT CODE`. Dimension B stays blocked for V05/V06/V07.**

🟡 **CANDIDATES.** External BTMM material uses *push* constantly and defines it loosely, in two
ways that do not fully agree:

- **(a) A directional burst / leg within a cycle.** "Dealers typically execute three price
  bursts or pushes during the day"; three is used because it is psychologically significant, and
  the third push terminates in stops being hit. Under this reading a push is a *structural leg*,
  counted, not measured.
- **(b) A sized unit derived from ADR.** External material repeats the same arithmetic the V06
  guest attributes to Steve — the ADR split into three, so each push ≈ ADR ÷ 3. Under this
  reading a push is a *distance*.

Neither external source supplies what the corpus actually lacks: **a rule for when a push has
started or finished on a live chart.** The general "market moves in three pushes" framing also
appears in trading writing well outside BTMM, which is a reason to trust it *less* as evidence
about this course specifically, not more.

**Note the trap:** reading (b) looks like it would unblock dimension B, because ADR ÷ 3 is
computable. It would not — it would replace a missing course rule with an internet rule and
then measure the internet rule. See §1.

### 5.2 — "level" (as a countable unit) · `A-004`

**Course status: UNCHANGED — `DO NOT CODE`.**

🟡 **CANDIDATES — and the external material carries the corpus's own ambiguity rather than
resolving it.** Three distinct external readings, all attested:

1. **An ordinal phase of the three-day cycle.** Level I = the fast market-maker-driven move,
   Level II = the retail-emotion-driven phase without MM support, Level III = the MM returning
   for profit-taking. This matches V02's slide printing `Level 1 / 2 / 3` as a sequence of legs.
2. **A moving-average relationship.** One widely-copied note renders it as
   *"LEVEL 1 — 13/50; LEVEL 2 — 50/200; LEVEL 3 — 50/800 or 200/800"*, i.e. a level is defined by
   which pair of EMAs price has cleared. This is a **third** reading, present in neither of the
   corpus's two, and if correct would tie `A-004` to `A-020` (the moving averages).
3. **A sustained run, qualitatively.** *"A level is a long sustained run, and days of
   consolidation should not be seen as levels"* — which is exactly the kind of unquantified
   phrasing this register exists to log.

The V01 visual evidence (a *horizontal dashed line on the template*) matches **none** of these
cleanly. That is a real disagreement and it is left standing.

### 5.3 — M and W formation, anatomy · `A-011`

**Course status: UNCHANGED — `DO NOT CODE`. This is the record `D-030` names first and it stays
open.**

🟡 **CANDIDATES.** External BTMM material treats M/W as the method's central shape and describes
it in **time**, not in candles:

- **Function:** *"'M' & 'W' patterns are the reversal footprint of market makers"*, formed at the
  low and high of the day.
- **Timing:** the gap between the two peaks of an M (or troughs of a W) *"will usually last for
  somewhere between 30 and 90 min (though occasionally longer)"*. **This is the same 30–90 minute
  figure the V06 guest reports at `[00:53:03]` alongside the nine-candle claim he then withdraws.**
  That correspondence is interesting and is **not** corroboration: it is the wider tradition and
  the corpus's guest quoting the same teacher, which is one source, not two.
- **Degenerate case:** a **railroad track** (one 15-minute up candle immediately followed by one
  15-minute down candle) is described as a *compressed M/W* — *"really an anomaly of an M or W
  pattern"*. If true, that would make the minimum "M" **two candles**, which contradicts any
  nine-candle floor outright.
- **Entry convention:** entry on the **second leg** of the M after a neckline break, with a
  13/50 EMA cross on M15 and a candle close below the 13 EMA.

**No external source gives a leg count, a proportion rule, or an invalidation condition** — the
three things `A-011` actually needs. The 30–90 minute window is a *typical duration*, not a
recognition rule, and importing it would be precisely the textbook-double-top substitution
`A-011`'s Required Research warns against.

**Course status: UNCHANGED — `DO NOT CODE`.**

🟢 **CONVERGENT on the object, 🔴 UNCLEAR on the rule.** Externally, the *second leg* is

### 5.5 — TDI (Traders Dynamic Index) · `A-039`

**Course status: UNCHANGED — `DO NOT CODE — and DO NOT SUBSTITUTE`. Condition (c) of V04's entry
rule is still unexecutable. `V04_MASTERY_REPORT.md` dimension G stays `DEFERRED`.**

🟢 **CONVERGENT on what TDI is** (it is a public MT4 indicator, not course IP), **🟡 CANDIDATES
on the settings BTMM uses.**

**The indicator, generically:** a composite oscillator combining RSI, moving averages of the RSI,
and volatility bands. Four plotted components, consistently named across sources:

| Line | Common name | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| Green | RSI Price Line | the RSI itself — short-term strength |
| Red | Trade Signal Line | a smoothing of the green line — direction |
| Blue (pair) | Volatility Band | expansion/contraction envelope around the RSI |
| Yellow | Market Base Line | the overall trend reference |

Levels are typically drawn at **68 / 50 / 32** (buying exhaustion / midpoint / selling exhaustion).

**⚠️ The settings do not agree, and this is the part that would matter for automation.** One
walkthrough gives RSI period **13**, with a period-**2** green MA and a period-**7** red MA;
the same source notes that *"some set the RSI period to 21, like in the BTMM TDI in the BTMM
Installer used by Steve Mauro"* — i.e. **the BTMM-specific build may differ from the generic
TDI**, and the corpus's own frame prints the panel title `TDI_MMM`, a custom build whose
parameters are not legible. Band deviation and applied-price settings were not recoverable at all.

**This is the sharpest illustration of why this file is reference-only.** A session could wire up
a public TDI in an afternoon. The result would be *a* TDI, not *the* `TDI_MMM` on the
instructor's chart, and every backtest of V04's three-condition rule would silently be a test of
a different indicator. `A-039`'s prohibition is unchanged and binds harder now, not less.

[TrendSpider — Introduction to TDI](https://trendspider.com/learning-center/introduction-to-tdi/) ·

### 5.8 — ADR and its lookback window · `A-038`, `A-040`

**Course status: UNCHANGED — `DO NOT CODE`.**

🔴 **UNCLEAR — and the external record is, if anything, *worse* than the corpus's.**

The **concept** is standard and undisputed: ADR = Average Daily Range, the mean daily high-to-low
excursion, used in BTMM both as a **gate** (is today's range near its usual full range?) and as a
**sizing unit** (ADR ÷ 3 per push / per level).

**The lookback period was not recoverable from any source.** Specifically:

- The commercial "BTMM Average Daily Range" MT4/MT5 indicator's product page documents that it
  *"show[s] the ADR and 3×ADR"* and marks ADR High/Low — **and states no default period at all.**
- Generic variable-lookback ADR scripts default to values like **7**, which is a script author's
  choice and says nothing about BTMM.
- No BTMM source found states 5, 10, 20 or any other window.

So the corpus's three incompatible bases (2 previous days / an unbounded "generally runs" average
/ whatever the template's `%DADR` reports) are **not** an artifact of poor transcription — the
ambiguity appears to be real and inherited. `A-038`'s Required Research (an instructor lesson, or
a frame with the indicator's settings dialog open) remains the **only** route, and this file
should be taken as evidence that searching externally for it again is not worth the time.

### 5.9 — "the Asian box" / Asian range · relates to V06 `[01:09:43]`, `A-037`, `A-019`

**Course status: UNCHANGED — undefined in-corpus; the V06 audience uses the phrase and the
presenter answers about push counting without ever defining the box.**

🟡 **CANDIDATES — the object is agreed, the boundaries are not.**

Agreed externally: the Asian session is BTMM's **accumulation** phase; the "box" is a rectangle
drawn on the session's **high, low and midpoint**, and it is the range that the London-open stop
hunt breaks out of falsely. Commercial BTMM session-box indicators draw Asian boxes, Brinks
boxes, New York boxes and stop-hunt boxes as a standard set, which confirms the box is a real
charting object in this tradition and not a figure of speech.

**Not agreed / not found:** the exact clock boundaries of the box. Descriptions place the
stop-hunt breakout *"between 1am and 4am EST"* and describe the market maker breaking the Asian
range *"in 3 swipes"*, then holding the new extreme for **30–90 minutes** — but the session
start/end times themselves vary by source and by broker feed. **This collides directly with
`A-019` / `D-031`**: any Asian box has a timezone assumption baked into it, and `D-031` requires
the timezone be *tested in two arms*, never assumed. An external box definition would smuggle in
exactly the assumption `D-031` forbids.

### 5.10 — "Hi-Lo" (HOD / LOD entry) · `A-056`

**Course status: UNCHANGED — `DO NOT CODE — the target is computable, the method is absent.`
`BT_V07_0001` measured the ceiling and explicitly does not close this record.**

🟢 **CONVERGENT — and it confirms `A-056`'s own diagnosis exactly.** External BTMM material
agrees on all of the following:

- The **object** is trivial: the high or low of the day, with `Pips To HOD` / `Pips To LOD`
  panels a standard part of the template — matching the corpus's V07 frames.
- The **HOD/LOD is created by the stop hunt**, typically 01:00–04:00 ET: an intentional move
  outside the range to what *becomes* the day's extreme, whose purpose is to take stops and
  induce false-direction commitments.

  established.

**And that last point is precisely the gap `A-056` records.** Every external source describes
recognising the extreme **retrospectively**; none states how to identify it *before it is known*,
which is the entire content of V07's *"get in… within a few pips"* claim. The wider tradition has
the same hole. `A-056`'s Required Research — whether "Jim" or any later lesson states the method
— is unchanged and is now known to be unanswerable from public sources.
