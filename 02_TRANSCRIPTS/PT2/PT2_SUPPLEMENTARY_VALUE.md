## 3. THE CANDIDATE RULES, BY GAP

### 3.1 ⭐ `A-010` / `A-056` — prospective PFH/PFL. **The strongest single contribution.**

The gap says a completed week's extreme is arithmetic but real-time finality is not. Pt 2 supplies
a **fully prospective** construction requiring no future bars:

> **`3. Cycles L2` [09:17]** *"This is our psychological support and resistance, our PSR, **which we
> box off from the Asian session on the one hour time frame.**"*
> **`3. Cycles L1` [35:19]** *"you draw out your PSR… **boxing in the first Asian session of the
> week**"* (repeated `3. Cycles L3` [24:00])
> **`3. Cycles L3` [06:37]** *"**Always note that your PSR will always be drawn before analyzing
> anything.**"*
> **`3. Cycles L2` [16:26]** *"**this is the strongest support and resistance that you'll ever see**…
> our spike candles will spike exactly to the PSR"*
> **`3. Cycles L1` [30:18]** *"what you expect is an **induction**… you had your PSR"*

**Candidate rule:** box the week's **first Asian session** on H1 → extend forward → the market
maker's false move off that box (*the induction*) is the week's stop hunt, **and that becomes the
peak formation.** Drawn before analysis; no lookahead.

⚠️ **A construction discrepancy to settle before coding.** The e-book says *"first **8 H1 candles**
of the trading week"*; the videos say *"first **Asian session** of the week."* Per `4. Timing L1`
[02:06]/[04:13] the Asian session is **8:30 p.m. – 3 a.m. NY** (~6.5 h) while the week opens at
**5 p.m. Sunday** — so the two definitions **do not produce the same box** unless the 5–8:30 p.m.
dead gap is skipped. One author, two constructions. **Do not average them; pick one, state which,
test both.**

⚠️ And the caution from `MMM_PT2` §2 stands: the BEEKAY source asserts *"nobody knows when a reset
will occur — even Steve Mauro & DMR Team can't predict them,"* and Mauro himself says *"I have no
idea"* about second legs (X17 [11:43]). **A confident T6 construction does not settle a question two
better-placed sources say is not settleable.** Test it; do not believe it in advance.

### 3.2 `A-138` — Level 1/2/3 quantification. Two independent-looking methods

**(a) EMA-cross markers** — the same scheme the X-series gives, stated more crisply:

> **`6. Exclusive Webinar` [06:18]** *"a **crossover of the 13 and the 50**, indication that it's

> and 13** — this is **peak formation**, level 3"*
> **`6. Exclusive Webinar` [46:33]** *"this right here is **level 2, just because of the EMAs**"*

**(b) ⭐ ADR boxes — a distance-based level count, and this is new to the corpus:**

> **`6. Exclusive Webinar` [07:24]** *"I take **ADR 57**, and I **create boxes** for one day of the
> ADR"*
> **`5. Using EMAs and ADR` [28:46]** *"if they already moved the ADR once, then you can expect them
> to start **accumulating level one around the 1× ADR**"*
> **`5. Using EMAs and ADR` [29:54]** *"once they have reached **ADR 3×**… we can start looking for
> **level three behavior** and that peak formation"*
> **`5. Using EMAs and ADR` [41:50]** *"**Level 2 occurs almost around the end of this ADR box**"*
> **`6. Exclusive Webinar` [05:10]** *"if the pair from the peak formation **exceeds the ADR 1× in
> one day, that means it's locked in**"*

**Candidate rule:** stack 1× ADR boxes from the peak formation; L1 ≈ end of box 1, L2 ≈ end of box
2, L3 at 3× ADR. This is **fully codable** and gives an independent cross-check on the EMA method —
the two can be tested against each other for agreement, which is a genuinely useful experiment and
needs no new material.

### 3.3 The reset — defined, with a negative example

> **`3. Cycles L1` [20:08]** *"a reset is basically when **price opens for the day and closes that
> day almost at the same price point, but we have a stop hunt high AND a stop hunt low**"*
> **`3. Cycles L6` [12:19]** *"what makes a reset is like a **stop hunt down and up**. So for example,
> **this would NOT be a reset** because even though we have a stop hunt high and low, **look at
> where price opened and closed.**"*
> **`3. Cycles L3` [23:00]** *"this could possibly be a reset and **this trend may continue**"*

⭐ **The negative example is the valuable part** — a stated non-instance is what makes a rule
testable, and it is the thing the corpus most often lacks. Note this **adds a condition** to the
e-book's version (`MMM_PT2` §3: *"closes more or less where it opened"*): both stop hunts are
required. Same author, more specific on video.

## 5. ⚠️⚠️ THE TRAP — WHY "PT 2 AGREES WITH MAURO" PROVES ALMOST NOTHING

`SOURCING_HIERARCHY.md` §1.3 records the exact failure mode for Tier 2 vs. Tier 3: the Tier-2 PDF is
probably the **upstream original** the Tier-3 web material was copied from, so *"Tier 3 agreeing with
Tier 2 is **one document quoted twice** — it is not independent corroboration and must never be
described as such."*

**That trap applies here with full force, and more strongly**, because the author states the
dependency himself: he learned the method from Mauro (`[01:06]`) and built a simplification of it
(`[02:09]`). So when Pt 2's session clock matches X05's, or Pt 2's 13/50 level-one marker matches
X19's, **that is a student reciting his teacher, not two witnesses agreeing.**

The same caution covers the **BEEKAY PDFs**: `MMM_PT2` §3 credits BEEKAY with the EMA-cross level
scheme, and Nick states the same scheme. Both are T6, both downstream of the same source, and
`BEEKAY_FX` refers to *"Steve Mauro & DMR Team"* in the third person — a separate group, but not an
independent one.

**What agreement here IS good for:** it raises confidence that the *transmission* is faithful — that
this is what the community understood Mauro to mean — which is useful for deciding **which
interpretation to test first**. It is not evidence that the rule is **true**, and it is not evidence
about what **Mauro** said. Only Tier 1 is evidence of that.
