## MANDATORY SCOPE STATEMENT (`PT-034` §8, verbatim)

> **PT-034 measures a claim whose headline form is arithmetically guaranteed.** Within a trading
> day, an entry within `X` pips of that day's extreme cannot draw down more than `X`, so *"Risk
> Reward to 3:1 or greater"* is true by construction for any `X ≤ 16.67` pips and carries no
> information about the market as stated. The empirical arms measure what is left: whether the
> 50-pip target is reached, how much of the tolerance is given back, and whether the geometry
> survives once the day's floor is removed.

> **It is not a test of a tradable rule.** V08 describes where to enter (the extreme), where in
> the structure (the second leg, at a prior trap area) and what cues it (speed) — but *second

> the matched-random null measures **the value of the missing skill**, and may never be quoted as
> evidence that the Hi-Lo claim is correct.
>
> Nothing here bears on any other V08 statement. `A-060`'s frequency claims remain blocked by
> `D-030` and are not touched by this test.

## 2. THE FIRST RESULT NEEDED NO DATA, AND IT IS THE MOST IMPORTANT ONE

Stated in `PT-034` §2 **before the run**, so it can never be presented as a discovery:

> Within a trading day, an entry within `X` pips of that day's extreme has maximum adverse
> excursion **`MAE ≤ X` by construction** — the extreme is the day's bound.

**Consequences, all arithmetic:**

- At the lesson's own printed tolerance `X = 10`, realised `R = 50 / MAE ≥ **5.0**` whenever the
  target is reached.
- **The 3:1 claim cannot fail within the day for any `X ≤ 16.67` pips.** The implied stop is
  unreachable.
- **So "Risk Reward to 3:1 or greater", read as stated with a within-day horizon, carries no
  information about the market.** It restates *if you enter near the low, your stop is near* —
  true of any instrument, in any regime, in any century.

**`O1` confirms the pipeline rather than the claim.** Pre-registered expectation: zero
observations with `MAE > X`. **Measured: 0 violations, in all 16 cell × `X` combinations.** That
is a self-test passing, not a finding, and it is labelled as such in the runner's own output.

**The measured `R` values are the bound, exactly.** Median realised `R` among target-reachers:
**25.00** at `X = 2`, **10.00** at `X = 5`, **5.00** at `X = 10` — i.e. exactly `50 / X` in every
cell. The claim's number is not merely met; it is met by construction and by a factor of 1.7 to
8.3.

## 6. ⚠ THE PRE-REGISTERED DECISION RULE HAS A DEFECT, AND IT IS VISIBLE ONLY NOW THAT THE NUMBER EXISTS

**The rule is not being changed.** `COMMON_PROTOCOL.md` §9 rule 7 forbids editing a
pre-registration to match what was found, and `PT-034` §6's table governs. The verdict above is
the verdict. **What follows is the defect, disclosed rather than absorbed.**

`PT-034` §4 warned, before the run:

> *"The rule arm uses hindsight: it knows where the day's extreme is. It is therefore expected to
> beat `N1`, and a high percentile is not evidence the claim is correct."*

**And then §6 made "above `N1`'s 95th percentile" a CONDITION for `CONFIRMED AS TAUGHT`.** Those
two sentences are in tension, and the run makes the tension concrete: the percentile came back
**100.0 in all four cells**, which the same file had already predicted was near-tautological.

**So one of the two conditions the verdict rests on carries almost no information.** The verdict
is effectively decided by the other one — the rate clearing 0.25 — which it does by a wide margin
(0.70 vs 0.25) and which is a real result.

**Why the `N1` condition was written in, and why it was not simply wrong.** §4's stated purpose
was falsifiable: *"if a random entry with the same geometry resolves to target at the same rate
as an entry at the day's extreme, then the crown jewel is the geometry, not the drill."* That is
a genuine test and it could have failed — `N1` could have come back at 0.70. **It did not; it
came back at 0.243.** The condition did work; what it did not do is discriminate, because the
hindsight advantage is so large that no plausible result would have failed it.

**The honest reading, stated at the strength the evidence supports:**

1. **The 3:1 geometry is not the finding.** It is arithmetic (§2), and against a random entry it
   is very slightly worse than break-even (§5 nulls).
2. **The finding is the size of the hindsight advantage: ~24.3% → 70.5–76.8%, a factor of 2.9–3.2.**
3. **That advantage is entirely attributable to knowing where the day's extreme is** — which is
   precisely what V07 named, V08 partially describes, and **neither teaches** (`A-056`,
   `A-061`).

**The verdict word `CONFIRMED AS TAUGHT` should therefore be read narrowly and with §2 attached:
what is confirmed is that a perfect-hindsight entry near the day's extreme, with the lesson's own
target and its own implied stop, resolves favourably far more often than break-even. What is
NOT confirmed — and cannot be tested — is that a trader can find that entry in advance.**

**Recommendation to the reviewer**, offered as a finding against this test's own design rather
than against V08: a decision rule whose second condition is a comparison the same file calls
near-tautological should not have been written. A better second condition would compare the rule
arm against a **non-hindsight** benchmark — e.g. an entry at the extreme of the *first half* of
the day, which is knowable in real time. **That is a new test, not an edit to this one.**
