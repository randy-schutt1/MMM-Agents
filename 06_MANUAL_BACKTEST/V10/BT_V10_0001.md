## 3. `M2` — THE FRIDAY CLOSE, 25–50 PIPS OFF BOTH EXTREMES

> `[00:13:41]`–`[00:13:52]` *"the dealer will end **always** 25 to 50 pips off of the high **and**
> 25 to 50 pips off of the low"*

**n = 178 Fridays** (180 usable weeks − 2 Thursday-closing weeks, excluded **by name**:
2015-12-20 and 2015-12-27, which have no Friday session and therefore cannot test a claim about
Friday).

| | Arm A | Arm B |
|---|---|---|
| median distance from Friday's **high** | 51.1 pips | 50.6 |
| median distance from Friday's **low** | 40.1 pips | 43.0 |
| **`O3` JOINT — both in [25, 50]** | **13 / 178 = 7.30%** · boot95 [3.93, 11.24] | **10 / 178 = 5.62%** · boot95 [2.25, 8.99] |
| marginal — from the high only | 46 / 178 = 25.84% | — |
| marginal — from the low only | 47 / 178 = 26.40% | — |

### Verdict — `M2`: **CONTRADICTED AS STATED**

`§6`'s threshold was `< 20% → CONTRADICTED`. **7.30% (Arm A) and 5.62% (Arm B).** Under the
literal reading of the speaker's own word *"always"*, which requires 100%, it is **13 of 178.**

### `N2` — the matched uniform-close null, and this is the more damaging result

| | Arm A | Arm B |
|---|---|---|
| expected joint rate under a uniform close, **matched per-Friday on that Friday's own range** | 7.12% | 7.00% |
| **observed** | 7.30% | 5.62% |
| **observed − null** | **+0.19 pp** | **−1.38 pp** |

> **`M2a` carries essentially NO information beyond the width of Friday's range.** A close placed
> **at random** inside Friday's own high–low interval lands in the stated band about as often as
> the real close does. On **Arm B the real close does it LESS often than chance.**
>
> This is what the null was for. Without it, "7.3%" reads as a weak-but-nonzero effect. With it,
> **the effect is zero**, and the small positive figure is entirely explained by the arithmetic
> fact that a ~75-pip Friday satisfies a 25–50-from-both-ends band almost automatically.

### `O5` / `N3` — the specificity control: **is Friday special at all?**

The claim's stated mechanism is weekend-specific — *"to trap the traders going into the weekend and
hit them with the gap."* Arm A:

| Session day | n | joint 25–50 | median d_high | median d_low | median range |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mon | 180 | 10.00% | 36.8 | 34.2 | 93.7 |
| Tue | 180 | 8.33% | 47.4 | 40.7 | 102.8 |
| Wed | 178 | 6.74% | 45.1 | 49.8 | 111.1 |
| **Thu** | 179 | **13.41%** | 40.6 | 47.6 | 100.2 |
| **Fri** | **178** | **7.30%** | 51.1 | 40.1 | 107.0 |

| | Arm A | Arm B |
|---|---|---|
| Friday − best other day | **−6.10 pp** | **−8.91 pp** |

### Verdict — `M2` specificity: **FAILED, and failed in the wrong direction**

`§6` required Friday to exceed **every** other day by ≥ 10 pp. **Friday does not exceed any of
them — it ranks fourth of five, and Thursday satisfies the band nearly twice as often.**

> **This is the finding with the most content in the whole test.** Even if one ignored the
> magnitude failure and the null, the claim would still be unsupported, because **whatever weak
> tendency exists for a daily close to sit 25–50 pips inside the day's extremes is not a Friday
> phenomenon.** The stated mechanism — trapping traders *into the weekend* — predicts a Friday
> effect specifically, and there is none.

### `O4` — the DERIVED implication. **THIS INFERENCE IS THE AGENT'S, NOT THE LESSON'S**

`V10_INTERPRETATION.md` Q2: if the close is 25–50 from both extremes, Friday's range is confined to
50–100 pips. **The speaker never says this**; `M2`'s verdict above is taken from `O3` alone.

| | Value |
|---|---|
| Fridays with range ∈ [50, 100] | 72 / 178 = **40.45%** |
| median Friday range | **107.0 pips** |

The median Friday is slightly wider than the derived band's ceiling — **consistent with the joint
failure, and it locates the cause**: the band is too narrow for a typical GBP/USD Friday.

## 8. ⚠ PROCESS DISCLOSURE — THE RUNNER WAS FIXED BETWEEN COMMIT AND EXECUTION

**Disclosed rather than absorbed, because the order of commits is the evidence this project runs on.**

The runner committed at `e53409e` **crashed on first execution** with an `IndexError` and produced
**no result of any kind**. The cause: it indexed session days as `(tm − week_anchor) // 1440` and
labelled the Sunday-17:00 → Monday-17:00 span *"day 0 = Sunday"*. Under `mmm_lib`'s convention
**C-1**, that span **is session day MONDAY**. The consequence was an **empty Friday set**, and
`np.percentile` on an empty array raised.

**What was done:** the session-day label is now derived from `mmm_lib.session_day` — the batch's own
machinery — rather than from an offset. The fix is committed **separately**, with this reasoning, and
the docstring of `sessions_for_week` records the error in place.

**Why this is not a `COMMON_PROTOCOL.md` §9 rule 7 violation.** Rule 7 forbids editing a runner *or*
a pre-registration to resolve a disagreement **discovered after a result exists** — the situation in
`BT_V08_0001` and `BT_V09_0001`, where the runner's decision logic was wrong and the numbers had
already been printed. **Here no number was ever produced.** The runner did not compute a wrong
answer; it computed nothing. **Nothing in `PT-036` was changed** — not a threshold, not a
prediction, not an exclusion, not the seed.

**And the failure mode is worth keeping.** An off-by-one in a weekday label is exactly the class of
error that normally passes silently and shifts every result by one day. **It did not pass silently
here**, because the mis-labelling emptied a set that the code then required to be non-empty. **A
loud crash on a labelling error is the good outcome**, and it is the same lesson `GOTCHA 4`,
`GOTCHA 5` and `D-020`'s retraction each record in different costumes: *build the check so the
wrong input cannot produce a well-formed output.*
