## 6. ⭐ `M1d` / `N3` — THE CONFOUND IS REAL, AND IT RUNS THE OTHER WAY

`PT-037` §2 named this as the bias most likely to manufacture a false confirmation, and built the
eligibility rule to remove its arithmetic form. **The rule worked and the correlation survived it.**

Arm A, low — margin over each stratum's own base rate, at `T = 30`:

| Stratum (candidate's minute-of-day) | `n` | median `R` remaining | `N1` | `P(F \| held ≥ 30)` | **margin** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 16:00–20:00 | 7,628 | **1,395 min** | 1.89% | 7.42% | **+5.53 pp** |
| 20:00–24:00 | 4,113 | 1,179 min | 1.51% | 8.90% | +7.39 pp |
| 00:00–04:00 | 4,466 | 862 min | 2.64% | 17.15% | +14.51 pp |
| 04:00–08:00 | 4,616 | 687 min | 3.83% | 24.31% | +20.48 pp |
| 08:00–12:00 | 4,016 | 425 min | 6.52% | 43.45% | +36.93 pp |
| 12:00–16:00 | 1,189 | **200 min** | 11.02% | 55.04% | **+44.02 pp** |

**The margin is monotone in remaining session time, across a factor of eight — from +5.5 pp to
+44.0 pp.** Sorted by median `R`, the six strata come out in exactly the right order with no
exceptions.

**Read plainly:** *where most of the session is still ahead, a low holding 30 minutes tells you
almost nothing (+5.5 pp). Where the session is nearly over, it tells you a great deal (+44 pp) —
and so would anything else, because there is no time left to break it.* The pooled +15.80 pp is a
weighted average of a large clock effect and, at best, a small duration effect.

**Verdict `PARTIALLY SUPPORTED`, and the qualification is the finding.** The margin clears 20 pp
in three strata and fails it in three, and which three is fully explained by remaining time.

## 7. ⭐ `N4` — AN ARBITRARY DAY BOUNDARY BEATS THE INSTRUCTOR'S

1,000 circular clock shifts, offsets uniform on ±12 h in 15-minute steps, seed `20260813`.

```text
true margin, arm A low, T=30           +15.80 pp
shifted-boundary null   n=1000         median +18.19 pp    5-95% [+15.66, +23.00]
the true margin sits at the             6.40th percentile
```

**The real 17:00 session boundary produces a SMALLER margin than 93.6% of arbitrary 24-hour
boundaries.**

This is a negative result about the framing, not only about the numbers, and it is the single
sharpest thing in this observation. If the instructor's day boundary carried structure, the true
margin should sit *high* in this null. **It sits near the bottom.** Whatever `M1a` is measuring, it
is not a property of *his* session day.

> **Stated as a limitation of my own inference, not smoothed away:** the null's own median is
> +18.19 pp, so **every** boundary produces a large margin. That is consistent with the whole
> effect being the generic mechanical one `N3` identifies. `N4` does not isolate a cause; it
> removes one candidate explanation — *"the 5 pm boundary is doing the work"* — and it removes it
> decisively.

## 9. `D-031` — BOTH ARMS, ALWAYS REPORTED, AND ONE OF THEM HAS A PROBLEM

| Cell | `N1` | `T = 30` | `T = 90` | `T = 120` | session days |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A, low** | 3.43% | 19.24% | 31.41% | 35.76% | **894** |
| A, high | 3.54% | 19.56% | 31.55% | 35.53% | 894 |
| **B, low** | 3.21% | 18.24% | 29.80% | 34.07% | **777** |
| B, high | 3.61% | 19.74% | 31.04% | 34.80% | 777 |

**Arms agree within 1.7 pp at every named threshold; low and high agree within 1.5 pp.**

> ### ⚠ ARM B'S SAMPLE IS DEGRADED BY 117 SESSION DAYS, AND THE CAUSE IS STRUCTURAL
>
> **Arm A excludes 11 session days on the completeness rule. Arm B excludes 245.** That is not a
> data defect; it is what `D-031` Arm B *does* to a day-boundary test, and it was not anticipated
> in `PT-037` §5.
>
> Arm B shifts stamps `+1 h` during US DST. The Friday close therefore lands at 18:00 and the
> Sunday open at 18:00 on the arm's own clock. Consequently, **for every DST week**:
>
> - the **Monday** session day `[Sun 17:00, Mon 17:00)` loses its first hour → **92/96 buckets →
>   EXCLUDED**;
> - a **Saturday** session day `[Fri 17:00, Sat 17:00)` materialises holding the stray Friday
>   17:00–18:00 hour → **4/96 → EXCLUDED**.
>
> The exclusion list shows exactly this alternating `92/96, 4/96` pattern from 2013-03-11 onward.
>
> **Consequence, stated rather than buried: Arm B's result rests on a sample missing most
> DST-period MONDAYS.** Since `N3` shows the effect varies strongly by time-of-day, a systematic
> loss of one weekday is a real threat to comparability. **The two arms nonetheless agree to
> within 1.7 pp**, which is reassuring but is *not* the clean robustness check `D-031` intends.
>
> **Arm A is the primary cell for every verdict in §3**, as `PT-037` §5 pre-registered. **This is
> recorded as a defect in `PT-037`'s design, found at run time, not as a result** — and it is
> flagged for the reviewer and for the `PT` batch generally, since **any future day-boundary test
> inherits it.**

## 12. MANDATORY SCOPE STATEMENT — what this result does and does not license

- **The speaker is the course author.** Under `D-033` no speaker-based fence applies.
- ⭐ **THE OPERATIONALISATION IS A NARROWING, DISCLOSED BEFORE THE RUN** (`PT-037` §8). The
  instructor's *"the low"* is a candidate low **made after a stop hunt, out of the blue box**.
  `A-076` blocks that filter, so **every running session-day M1 low was tested instead** —
  **26,028 of them, against the ~4–9 a chart reader would mark.** Therefore:
  - **`M1c`'s contradiction does NOT show his rule fails.** It shows that on *unfiltered* candidate
    lows, 30 and 90 minutes are not distinguished points. His filtered version is untested.
  - **`M1b`'s confirmation does NOT validate his setup either.**
- **`FINAL` means final within the `C-1` session day** (17:00→17:00). Not *"the low of the move"*,
  not the weekly low.
- **No trade was simulated.** No stop, no target, no position size, no R:R. **Nothing here bears
  on profitability.**
- **No RSI threshold was tested** (§1). V11's most codable-looking content is entirely absent from
  this result.
- **GBP/USD only** (`D-007`). **2013–2016 only**; the lesson was recorded **2012-04-08** and the
  corpus does not reach 2012 at any usable resolution — a real external-validity limit, stated
  before the result and repeated here.
- **Price levels are not comparable with the V02–V06 FXCM homework** (`D-036a`). **`PT-037` reads
  no price level at all** — only orderings and clock differences — so it is structurally immune.

## 13. RUNNER-VS-PRE-REGISTRATION DISAGREEMENTS

`COMMON_PROTOCOL.md` §9 rule 7: if they disagree, **the pre-registration governs and neither is
edited**; the disagreement is reported here. It has fired three times before in this project,
every time against the runner.

**This time: no substantive disagreement. Two items are recorded anyway.**

1. **The candidate detector was vectorised.** `PT-037` §3.0 specifies *"strictly below the running
   minimum"*. The runner uses `np.minimum.accumulate` and tests `run[i] != run[i-1]`, which changes
   value **only** on a strictly lower low and is therefore the same predicate. **The reason it
   matters is procedural**: the first, scalar implementation made `N4`'s 1,000 rebuilds
   computationally infeasible, and the tempting fix was to quietly cut the iteration count. **The
   pre-registered control was kept and the code was made faster instead**, which is the choice that
   should be visible in the record.
2. **`PT-037` §5 predicted the two arms would differ but did not anticipate the mechanism.** §9's
   Arm-B Monday loss is a **design gap found at run time**. It is reported as a defect in the
   pre-registration, **not corrected in it**.
