# BT_V07_0001 — PT-033: the Hi-Lo ceiling, and the size of the untaught gap

> ## ⚠ MANDATORY SCOPE STATEMENT — `PT-033` §8, verbatim
>
> **PT-033 measures the arithmetic ceiling of an entry at the day's true extreme, using
> hindsight.** It is **not** a test of a tradable rule: V07 names "Hi-Lo" as a primary method,
> recommends it in print, and does **not** teach how to identify the extreme in real time
> (`A-056`), so no version of this test can be executed forward. A high ceiling does **not**
> support the claim; it establishes only that the claim is arithmetically possible. The rule
> arm's advantage over the matched-random-entry null is a measure of **the value of the missing
> skill**, and may never be quoted as evidence that the Hi-Lo claim is correct. Nothing here
> bears on any other V07 statement, all of which remain blocked by `D-030`.

> ### ⚠ THE PERCENTILE IS 100.0 IN EVERY CELL, AND IT MEANS NOTHING

## 6. THE DAY BOUNDARY IS LOAD-BEARING, AND THE TIMEZONE ARM IS NOT — DISCLOSED SENSITIVITY

The four cells look like they disagree on two axes. They disagree on one.

**A pre-registered rule interacted with Arm B in a way `PT-033` did not anticipate.** The
corpus's week **closes** at 17:00 fixed EST (`D-036a`); under Arm B that same instant is stamped
**18:00** during US DST, so the last hour of each DST Friday falls past the 17:00 day boundary
and forms a **4-bar stub "day"**. Those stubs are legitimate under `PT-033` §7's pre-registered
*"drop days with fewer than 4 bars"* rule — they have exactly four — and they are why cell
`B · D-SESSION` holds **1,021** days where `A · D-SESSION` holds **904**.

`06_MANUAL_BACKTEST/V07/sensitivity_pt033.py`, run and reported as **post hoc and labelled as
such**:

| Cell | days at ≥4 bars (pre-registered) | stub days (4–7 bars) | stub median range | days at ≥8 bars | `f50_day` at ≥8 bars |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A · D-SESSION | 904 | **0** | — | 904 | 0.9535 |
| A · D-MIDNIGHT | 1,087 | **0** | — | 1,087 | 0.8096 |
| **B · D-SESSION** | 1,021 | **118** | **15.0 p** | 903 | **0.9546** |
| B · D-MIDNIGHT | 1,087 | **0** | — | 1,087 | 0.8086 |

**Remove 118 one-hour Friday-evening stubs and `B · D-SESSION` moves from 0.8462 to 0.9546 — a
dead match for `A · D-SESSION`'s 0.9535.**

So the honest reading of §3:

> **The `D-031` timezone arm makes essentially no difference to this claim. The definition of
> "the day" makes a very large one: a 17:00 session day gives `f50_day ≈ 0.95`, a midnight day
> gives `≈ 0.81`. Fourteen percentage points, from a choice V07 never states.**

**Three disclosures that go with that sentence:**

1. **The headline result in §3 stands exactly as pre-registered.** The sensitivity is reported
   *alongside* it, not in place of it. `PT-033` is not edited (`COMMON_PROTOCOL.md` §9 rule 7).
2. **The verdict does not change.** Under the sensitivity rule the cells are 0.9535, 0.8096,
   0.9546, 0.8086 — still two above and two below 0.95, still `INDETERMINATE`.
3. **This is a `D-031`-shaped finding about a different variable.** `D-031` says a divergence
   between clock treatments is a finding, never a selection criterion. The same discipline is
   applied here to the day boundary, and **neither day definition is preferred**.

## 9. WHAT THIS OBSERVATION DOES AND DOES NOT ESTABLISH

**Does:**

- One claim from V07 has been pre-registered and tested on 904–1,087 days of GBP/USD, on both
  `D-031` arms and both day definitions, with 1,000-iteration nulls and a second vendor.
- **Verdict `INDETERMINATE`** under the pre-registered rule, because the cells straddle 0.95.
- **The ceiling is high**: on a 17:00 session day, **95.4%** of days contained a 50-pip run from
  the day's extreme; on a midnight day, **81.0%**.
- **`O2` collapses into `O1`.** At perfect hindsight, *"was a 50-pip run available"* and *"did
  the day have 50 pips of range"* are the same question, to three decimal places.
- **Tolerance is cheap**: 10 pips of entry imprecision costs 2.4–3.0 pp.
- **The untaught skill is worth roughly 0.29–0.37 in hit rate** against a matched random entry.
- **The day boundary is load-bearing (≈14 pp) and the timezone arm is not**, once 118 Arm-B
  Friday stub days are accounted for — corroborated by a second vendor whose day boundary falls
  between the two tested.
- **Random direction is indistinguishable from matched direction.** A clean null, reported.

**Does not:**

- Show that Hi-Lo trading works. **It cannot** — V07 supplies no method of finding the extreme
  in real time (`A-056`) and this test invents none.
- Show that Hi-Lo trading fails, for the same reason.
- Bear on any other V07 statement. Second legs, levels, M/W, railroad tracks, *tilted*, the
  *A pattern*, shark fin and TDI all remain blocked by `D-030`.
- Close any `A-xxx` or `C-xxx` record. **`A-056` is opened by this work, not closed by it.**
- Say anything about price **levels** — `D-036a` makes that unmeasurable for this window.
- Generalise beyond GBP/USD, 2013-01-06 → 2016-06-30, and a 50-pip target.
- Touch the holdout. `2016-07-01 → 2017-12-29` was never on disk.

## 9a. ADDENDUM 2026-08-13 (later, same day) — THE `C8` DATA HOLE, MEASURED AGAINST THIS RESULT

**A concurrent session added a QA check after this observation was published** and it found a
real defect in the corpus this test ran on (`f7c5c04`): **a ~22-hour hole at
`2014-05-30 16:59` → `2014-06-02 15:01`** — a missing Sunday open plus Monday's Asian and London
sessions, the only unexplained absence in 3.5 years. `D-036a`'s original `C5`–`C7` sign-off
passed it: `C6` excluded it by construction (its gap census skips anything ≥12 h as "the
weekend") and `C7` surfaced it as a cosmetic weekday-tally entry.

**This observation's QA-gate citation in §1 was therefore made against a gate that was one check
short, and that is disclosed here rather than left for a reviewer to find.**

**The effect on this result was measured, not assumed.** Exactly **one day bucket per cell** is
affected (`2014-06-02`), it is a **miss in all four cells**, and it is retained by `PT-033` §7's
pre-registered *"fewer than 4 bars"* drop rule because it holds 8, 36, 4 and 32 bars
respectively:

| Cell | bars in the affected bucket | `f50_day` as reported | `f50_day` excluding the bucket | Δ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A · D-SESSION | 8 | 0.9535 | 0.9546 | +0.0011 |
| A · D-MIDNIGHT | 36 | 0.8096 | 0.8103 | +0.0007 |
| B · D-SESSION | 4 | 0.8462 | 0.8471 | +0.0009 |
| B · D-MIDNIGHT | 32 | 0.8086 | 0.8094 | +0.0008 |

> **The verdict is unchanged.** 0.9546 / 0.8103 / 0.8471 / 0.8094 still straddle the 0.95
> boundary, so `PT-033` §6 still returns **`INDETERMINATE`**. Every headline figure in §§2–7
> stands as reported.
>
> **The reported numbers are NOT edited to the excluding-C8 column.** The pre-registered drop
> rule kept that day and the pre-registered rule is what this observation reports; the
> sensitivity is disclosed beside it, exactly as the Arm-B stub days are in §6.

**The general point is worth more than the arithmetic.** `D-036a` argued that a CSV read
satisfies `E06` *more* completely than a chart read. That remains true about **provenance** —
and this hole is the counter-consideration about **perception**: on a chart, 22 missing hours are
a visible discontinuity; in a column of numbers they compute silently into a result. **The QA
gate is the compensating control, and it was incomplete when this test cited it.** Anyone reusing
this corpus should cite the **post-`C8`** report, not the one recorded in `D-036a`.
