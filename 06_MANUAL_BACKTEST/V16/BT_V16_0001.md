## §4 — WHY THE CEILING READING BEATS THE TYPICAL READING

**Not because the ceiling is well supported — `WEAKLY SUPPORTED` is a 7% breach rate, and an
externally-imposed limit breached one day in fourteen is not much of a limit.** It wins because the
**TYPICAL** reading is worse: **`200` sits at roughly the 93rd–95th percentile of the daily range
distribution**, and `O5` shows the 99th percentile at **284–291 pips**, i.e. **days well past 200
happen every year in every year of both windows.**

`O4` is the sharpest single number: **only 17.5% of `W-D` days (11.6% of `W-E` days) fall inside a
band as generous as `150–250` pips.** Four days in five are outside a ±25% window around the
lesson's figure.

The claim's own words are *"do not exceed 200 pips **on the average day**"*. `N2` computes the
lesson's own averaging object — a trailing 15-day mean, `[00:09:31]`'s stated lookback — and
**`P(ADR₁₅ > 200) = 0.0045` in `W-D`**, i.e. the 15-day average range exceeded 200 pips on

`WEAKLY SUPPORTED`.

⚠ **THAT VERDICT IS NOT TAKEN, AND THE REASON MATTERS MORE THAN THE NUMBER.** `N2` was
pre-registered as a **control**, `O1` as the **outcome**, and §6 binds the verdict to `O1`.
**Swapping them after seeing which one flatters the claim is exactly the move pre-registration
exists to prevent.** It is offered to the reviewer as a **candidate design for a future test**
(`PT-045`, if anyone wants it), **not as a result.**

**`obs-2` — a corpus completeness anomaly in 2023.** `N3` shows **140** included days in 2023
against ~253 in every neighbouring year. Raw-bar sampling shows 2023 also carries ~12% fewer M1
rows than its neighbours, so incomplete days compound. **2023's median (90.6) sits inside its
neighbours' range, so it does not move any verdict** — but a year that loses 45% of its days to the
completeness filter is worth a reviewer's attention and is not something `PT-044` set out to find.

**`obs-3` — arm B loses a quarter of all days**, 23.9% (`W-D`) and 25.4% (`W-E`), against arm A's
1.2% and 8.0%. **This is the same structural loss `BT_V15_0001` named at week boundaries under the

the answer here**: every arm-B verdict equals its arm-A verdict in both windows, and `N1`'s median
deltas are 2.05 and 0.80 pips.

### §6a — ⚠ THE RUNNER'S FIRST EXECUTION DID NOT IMPLEMENT §4's `W-E`, AND ITS FIGURES ARE SUPERSEDED

`PT-044` §4 defines `W-E` as **2017-01-01 → 2025-12-31** and §4 forbids pooling
(*"reported separately and never averaged"*). `mmm_lib`'s `scope="extended"` loads **2013 → 2025**
— `W-D` **pooled with** the extension. **The first execution therefore reported a pooled window
under the label `W-E`.** The runner was corrected to clip to §4's dates, which **implements** the
pre-registration rather than deviating from it, and the run was repeated.

**The superseded pooled figures are recorded here rather than discarded:** arm A `n = 3072`,
`O1 = 0.0560`, `O2 = 95.1`, `O3 = 107.6`, `O4 = 0.1335`, `O5 = 285.9`. **Both verdicts are
unchanged from the corrected `W-E`** (CEILING `WEAKLY SUPPORTED`, TYPICAL `CONTRADICTED`), so no
conclusion in this file rests on the error — **but the error was real, it was in a committed
runner, and a reader is entitled to see both sets of numbers.**

### §6b — A DETAIL §3 DID NOT FIX, RESOLVED IN THE RUNNER AND DISCLOSED HERE

`PT-044` §3 fixes `ADR₁₅`'s **lookback length** (15) but not whether the 15 days are **calendar**
days or the **included** days of §3a. The runner uses the **ordinal sequence of included days**.
The two differ wherever §3a excluded a day — which, on arm B, is a quarter of them. **`N2` is a
control and no verdict depends on it, but the choice is stated here rather than left in the code.**
