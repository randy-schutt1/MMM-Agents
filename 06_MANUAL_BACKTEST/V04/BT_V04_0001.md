## 2. SAMPLE ACCOUNTING — INCLUSION, COMPLETENESS, AND WHAT THE QA GATE DID NOT SURFACE

### 2a. The completeness rule (convention C-6), and why it exists

`QA_REPORT.txt`'s C5–C7 sign-off is **not** a completeness guarantee. C6 counts only
*intra-week* gaps ≥ 30 minutes and reports three, totalling 4h43m. It **cannot see** a gap
that crosses a weekend, and there is one.

A completeness rule was therefore fixed **before its effect on any result was looked at**,
on completeness grounds only:

> **C-6.** A session day is **excluded** if any 15-minute bucket of its box window
> (26 buckets, 20:30 → 03:00) or of its post-box window (56 buckets, 03:00 → 17:00) is
> absent from the corpus. Exclusions are **counted and reported beside n**.

**Why the bucket and not the minute.** The tests read 15-minute bars, so full bucket
coverage means every slot the test reads exists. Requiring 100% M1 coverage would exclude
most of the corpus for something that is not a defect — a bid feed legitimately prints no
tick in a quiet minute. Among full-bucket days in W-B the M1 floor is 363/390 in the box and
741/840 in the post-box window, with 1st percentiles of 372/390 and 828/840: ordinary thin
minutes, not holes.

**The same rule is applied to the N2 null**, because a clock-shifted sham window that runs
off the end of the week is not measurable either, and scoring the null more loosely than the
rule arm would flatter whichever it favoured.

**The rule is applied identically to data defects and to real market closures.** A
half-covered Christmas Eve is a genuine closure *and* cannot support a full-window
measurement.

### 2b. The accounting

```text
n = 512  (3 excluded for incomplete sessions:
          2014-12-24  box 26/26, post 44/56   Christmas Eve early close
          2015-08-21  box 26/26, post 55/56   the 41-minute gap in QA C6
          2015-12-24  box 26/26, post 44/56   Christmas Eve early close)
```

Identical on both arms. **Every one of the 512 included days has all 26 box buckets and all
56 post-box buckets.** No day was excluded for being unrepresentative — no holiday filter, no
news filter, no volatility filter (`COMMON_PROTOCOL.md` §3 disclosure 1). The three above are
excluded for coverage, and named.

**Four further weekday session days never reached the C-6 test at all**, because they carry
no box or no post-box window whatsoever and so fail C-5:

| Absent session day | Cause |
|---|---|
| 2014-12-25, 2015-01-01, 2015-12-25 | Holiday closures — expected |
| **2014-06-02** | **A vendor coverage hole** — see below |

### 2c. The unexplained hole, and the QA metric that cannot see it

M1 data stops at `2014-05-30 16:59` and resumes at `2014-06-02 15:01` — **4,202 minutes**.
That removes the entire Sunday week open (2014-06-01: 0 bars against a normal ~399) and all
of Monday morning through 15:01 (521 bars against a nominal 1,440). It swallows Monday's
Asian and London sessions, so **session day 2014-06-02's box cannot be constructed at all**
and the day is absent rather than partial.

A full scan for gaps > 30 minutes that are **not** a standard Friday-close → Sunday-open
weekend finds **10** in 3.5 years. Nine are Christmas/New-Year closures. **One — the June
2014 hole — is not, and it is the only unexplained one in the corpus.**

> **This is a finding about the corpus and about the gate.** C6's intra-week test excludes
> anything crossing a weekend, so the largest coverage hole in the data does not appear in
> the QA report. Recorded here; **no `PT` file and no QA script is edited by this session**.

### 3a. The other honest cut, reported with equal prominence

Pooling both edges counts the *unbreached* edge of a one-sided day as a zero. The day's
**larger** excursion — one number per day — is the object a trader would actually meet:

| | Arm A | Arm B |
|---|---|---|
| Median | **51.4 pips** | **53.1 pips** |
| Modal 5-pip bin | `[40, 45)` (n = 35) | `[40, 45)` (n = 36) |
| 25 ≤ e ≤ 50 | 0.312 (160/512) [0.274, 0.354] | 0.311 (159/512) [0.272, 0.352] |
| 50 < e ≤ 100 | 0.359 (184/512) [0.319, 0.402] | 0.379 (194/512) [0.338, 0.422] |
| e > 100 | 0.160 (82/512) [0.131, 0.194] | 0.172 (88/512) [0.142, 0.207] |

**The median day's larger excursion is ~51–53 pips — just past the ceiling of the
instructor's band, not inside it.** Conditional on a breach at all (zeros dropped, n = 791 /
828), the median is 33.7 / 33.6 pips and the modal bin is `[10, 15)`.

So the band's status depends on which object you ask about, and **all three are reported
because choosing among them after seeing them is `E09`**:

- per **edge**, including unbreached edges → mode is 0–5, band holds ~20%;
- per **day**, larger side → median just *above* 50, band holds ~31%;
- per **breach** → median ~33, mode 10–15, band holds ~25%.

**None of the three makes 25–50 modal.** V02's own hedge at `[00:44:59]` —
*"maybe they'll only do 10 pips… then they'll do it 100 pips"* — describes this corpus
better than the V04 band does, and 8–9% of edge-observations exceed 100 pips.

| | Arm A | Arm B |

**Every one of 512 days cleared at least one edge of the Asian box by at least some amount.**
`PT-014` §3 Measure 3 was written to capture the *"it won't come out of the box and you'll be

A day that takes out the box high *and* the box low is a day on which "the direction of the
breach" is not a single fact — which is precisely the population `PT-021` was pre-registered
to split.

**Measure 4 — is the first excursion the larger one, and when do they happen?**

| | Arm A | Arm B |
|---|---|---|
| First-breached edge is also the larger excursion | 0.682 (349/512) [0.640, 0.721] | 0.646 (331/512) [0.604, 0.687] |

**73–74% of first breaches occur in the 03:00 hour — the very first hour after the box
closes.** That is a structural fact with consequences for every dependent test: the trigger
these tests wait for has usually already happened within one hour of the box's close, long
before the London or New York sessions are underway.

**Measure 5 — dimensionless, and the volatility-normalised second baseline**

| | Arm A | Arm B |
|---|---|---|

## 6. THE BASELINE. **N2 — CIRCULAR CLOCK SHIFT**

Computed and printed before the rule arm's aggregates were read, 1,000 draws, seed
`20260812`, both arms.

| Statistic | Arm A observed | Arm A null (median, 5–95%) | pct | Arm B observed | Arm B null | pct |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Median pooled excursion | 21.6 | 6.95, [0, 25.2] | **72.7** | 23.8 | 7.30, [0, 25.0] | **78.3** |
| Share with **no** excursion | 0.000 | 0.064, [0, 0.364] | **0.0** | 0.000 | 0.059, [0, 0.345] | **0.0** |
| Share in 25–50 (pooled) | 0.195 | 0.157, [0.071, 0.230] | **60.4** | 0.202 | 0.159, [0.072, 0.227] | **68.5** |
| Modal 5-pip bin (lower edge) | 0 | 0, [0, 0] | — | 0 | 0, [0, 0] | — |

**Three readings, and the third is the one that matters:**

1. **The 25–50 share is indistinguishable from the sham box.** At the 60th / 69th percentile of
   the null, the Asian window is not distinctively productive of 25–50 pip
   excursions. **Any 6.5-hour window placed anywhere on the clock produces about as many.**
2. **Median excursion is elevated but does not clear the null's 95th percentile** (72.7 /
   78.3). It leans the right way and it is not significant at the pre-registered bar.
3. **The share of no-excursion days is at percentile 0.0 on both arms, and this is the one
   real result in the baseline comparison.** The real box is breached on *every* day; a
   randomly-placed box is not breached at all on a median 6% of days and on up to 40% of days
   in the tail. **The Asian window's distinctive property is not the size of what follows it
   — it is that something always follows it.** That is what a quiet window sitting
   immediately before an active one looks like, and it is the strongest thing PT-014 finds.

`PT-014` §5 anticipated the reading that would go with (1):

> *"The distribution is indistinguishable from the sham-box control → **the Asian window has
> no distinctive excursion profile**, which would be a serious negative for the box concept
> itself."*

**That is half-true here and must be stated as a half.** The *magnitude* profile is
essentially generic (percentiles 60–78, neither clearing 95). The *breach-certainty* profile
is not generic at all. Reporting either half alone would be `E09`.

## 7. `D-031` — THE TWO ARMS, AND WHERE THEY DIVERGE

The arms agree on every headline: same modal bin `[0, 5)`, same zero no-excursion days, band
shares within 0.007 of each other, dimensionless ratios within 0.3 box-widths, same modal
first-breach hour.

**They disagree in two places, and both are findings, not defects (`D-031`).**

**(a) The measured box is a different box.** Median box range **31.0 pips (A)** vs **26.7
pips (B)** — a 14% difference from moving the window one hour. Everything conditioned on box
size inherits it, `PT-016` most directly.

**(b) A one-hour shift changed the largest single observation in the sample by 216 pips.**

| | Arm A | Arm B |
|---|---|---|
| Largest excursion in W-B | **397.6 pips**, 2015-03-18 | 222.7 pips, 2015-05-01 |
| 2015-03-18, high-side excursion | **397.6** (post-box high 1.51676) | **181.1** (post-box high 1.49511) |

2015-03-18 is an FOMC day. Arm A's post-box day runs to 17:00 file time; Arm B's runs to
17:00 New York time, which during US DST is **16:00 file time**. The rally's high fell in
that final hour, so Arm A sees it and Arm B does not. Same price path, same instrument, same
calendar day — **the day's headline number more than doubles depending on which reading of
"5pm" you take.**

> **This is `A-019`'s stakes made arithmetic**, in the same way `BT_V06_0001` §3b found them
> for session range. It is **not evidence about `A-019` itself** and may not be cited as
> such: the course prints no timezone, and both arms are this project's constructions.

**Stability across years, descriptive:** median pooled excursion 16.4 (2014) → 27.3 (2015) on
Arm A, 18.9 → 28.9 on Arm B. **The distribution moved substantially between two adjacent
years of the same window.** Any band stated in absolute pips is a statement about a
volatility regime as much as about a market; Measure 5's dimensionless form (0.603–0.604 of the
day's own range on both arms) is the one that did not move.

## 8. THE FORECAST PT-014 EXISTS TO PRODUCE — AND IT IS BAD NEWS FOR PT-015

`INDEX.md` §3: *"PT-014 runs first in its family because it reports the excursion
distribution the others select inside; discovering after the fact that a band selects a tail
is how a thin sample gets explained away instead of reported."*

The dependent tests classify a day by its **first 15-minute close** beyond a box edge, not by
the day's maximum. Those are very different objects when price walks out of the box
gradually — and it does:

| `PT-015` stratum (assigned by **first qualifying close**, as pre-registered) | Arm A | Arm B |
|---|---|---|
| **S1** 10–24 pips | 417 | 429 |
| **S2** 25–50 pips | **44** | **46** |
| **S3** 51–100 pips | **12** ⚠ | **9** ⚠ |
| **S4** > 100 pips | **0** ⚠ | **0** ⚠ |
| *(days with any first close ≥ 10 pips)* | *474/512 (0.926)* | *486/512 (0.949)* |

| Contrast — the same strata by the day's **maximum** excursion (**not** the pre-registered rule) | Arm A | Arm B |
|---|---|---|
| S1 10–24 · S2 25–50 · S3 51–100 · S4 > 100 | 71 · 160 · 184 · 82 | 59 · 159 · 194 · 88 |

> ### **`PT-015`'s S3 will be `SAMPLE INSUFFICIENT` and its S4 will be EMPTY. This is known
> before PT-015 runs, and it is a property of the pre-registration, not of the result.**
>
> Because price usually leaves the box gradually, the *first* close beyond an edge is almost
> always in the 10–24 band; a day only lands in S3 or S4 if it jumps more than 50 pips
> between two consecutive 15-minute closes. **`PT-015` §3 forbids merging strata after the
> fact** (*"Collapsing S3 and S4 because S4 came up thin would be choosing a cut after seeing
> the data, which is `E09` no matter how reasonable it looks at the time"*) — so PT-015 will
> run as written and report S3 and S4 as insufficient. That is the correct outcome and it is
> pre-announced here rather than discovered later.

## 10. WHAT THIS OBSERVATION DOES AND DOES NOT ESTABLISH

**Does:**
- The excursion distribution beyond the Asian box on GBP/USD in W-B is measured, both arms:
  **modal bin `[0, 5)` pips; 25–50 holds ~0.20 of edge-observations.**
- The instructor's band is **not modal** on any of the three honest cuts of the data.
- **Every one of 512 days cleared at least one box edge** — the "stays in the box" case is
  empty at this resolution.
- The Asian box's magnitude profile is **indistinguishable from a randomly-placed 6.5-hour
