### Costs are not modelled, and that direction matters

The corpus is **bid** data and no spread or slippage model is invented (same discipline as
`PT-017` §3). Every expectancy below is therefore an **upper bound**: a real short entered on
the bid and covered on the ask pays the spread twice. GBP/USD spread in this era is roughly
1–2 pips, against expectancies of −3.3 to +4.6 pips. **A reader must not treat a small
positive expectancy here as a positive one.**

## 2. THE SAMPLE, AND WHY THE CEILING QUESTION COULD NOT BE ANSWERED

### 2a. Completeness accounting (convention C-6), and the before/after it caused

```text
n = 512  (3 excluded for incomplete sessions:
          2014-12-24  box 26/26, post 44/56   Christmas Eve early close
          2015-08-21  box 26/26, post 55/56   the 41-minute gap in QA C6
          2015-12-24  box 26/26, post 44/56   Christmas Eve early close)
```

Identical on both arms; every included day has all 26 box and all 56 post-box buckets. The
rule, its rationale and the corpus coverage hole it was written for are in `BT_V04_0001` §2.
Nothing was excluded for being unrepresentative.

**C-6 was added after this test had already been computed once, and the before/after is
reported rather than absorbed:**

| | before C-6 | after C-6 |
|---|---|---|
| S1 n, A / B | 419 / 431 | **416 / 428** |
| S1 hit rate, A / B | 0.178 / 0.225 | **0.178 / 0.224** |
| S1 expectancy, A / B | −3.25 / −0.43 | **−3.29 / −0.52** |
| S1 percentile vs N1 (exp), A / B | 0.3 / 38.1 | **0.2 / 36.2** |
| S2 n, hit, exp — both arms | unchanged | **unchanged** |
| S3 n, hit, exp — both arms | unchanged | **unchanged** |
| S2 − S1 permutation p, A / B | 0.187 / 0.239 | **0.205 / 0.240** |

**No verdict moved.** The three excluded days all fell in S1, which is the only stratum whose
figures changed at all.

### 2b. The strata


474 (Arm A) / 486 (Arm B) of 512 included session days produced a first close ≥ 10 pips
beyond a box edge. Their assignment to the four pre-registered strata:

| Stratum | Arm A | Arm B |
|---|---|---|
| **S1** 10 ≤ e < 25 | **416** | **428** |
| **S2** 25 ≤ e ≤ 50 | **44** | **46** |
| **S3** 50 < e ≤ 100 | **12** ⚠ | **9** ⚠ |
| **S4** e > 100 | **0** ⚠ | **0** ⚠ |

> ### **This shortfall was predicted, in writing, before this test ran.**
>
> `BT_V04_0001` §8 reported exactly these counts as PT-014's forecast, with the mechanism:
> price usually leaves the box **gradually**, so the *first* close beyond an edge is almost
> always in the 10–24 band. A day reaches S3 only by jumping more than 50 pips between two
> consecutive 15-minute closes, and S4 only by jumping more than 100.
>
> **The consequence is structural, not statistical.** PT-015 asks whether >50-pip excursions
> behave differently from 25–50-pip excursions. Under its own pre-registered assignment rule,
> **>50-pip *first closes* barely exist.** The test as written cannot reach n ≥ 30 in the
> stratum whose behaviour is the question.
>
> **Nothing was changed to fix this.** `PT-015` §3 is explicit: *"Collapsing S3 and S4 because
> S4 came up thin would be choosing a cut after seeing the data, which is `E09` no matter how
> reasonable it looks at the time."* S3 and S4 are reported as they are. Re-issuing the test
> with an assignment rule based on the day's **maximum** excursion — which `BT_V04_0001` §8
> shows would give S1/S2/S3/S4 = 71/160/184/82 on Arm A — would be **a different test needing
> a new `PT` number under `D-027`**, and this session does not write one.

## 5. THE SECOND AND THIRD BASELINES

**Second — S1 as the course's own low-side comparator (`PT-015` §4).**

| | Arm A | Arm B |
|---|---|---|
| Mean expectancy S2 − S1 | +5.28 pips | +5.11 pips |
| Permutation two-sided p (1,000 shuffles, seed `20260812`) | **0.205** | **0.240** |
| Hit rate S1 vs S2 | 0.178 vs 0.268 | 0.224 vs 0.282 |

**S1 and S2 are not distinguishable at this sample on either arm.** `PT-015` §5 names this
outcome and says what it means:

> *"S1 behaves like S2 → **The floor is the weaker part of the criterion**, which would be a
> surprise worth reporting prominently — it is the half `V04_INTERPRETATION.md` §3.2 says
> rests on a single prescriptive utterance."*

**Reported prominently, as instructed.** The test was designed to interrogate the *ceiling*
of "25 to 50"; the sample it actually got says nothing survives about the **floor** either.
The point estimates lean the instructor's way on both arms (+5 pips), and the permutation
test cannot separate that from noise.

**Third — volatility-matched S2 vs S3. "The third arm decides the test."**

| | Arm A | Arm B |
|---|---|---|
| Matched pairs formed | 12 | 9 |
| Median &#124;prior-TR difference&#124; within a pair | 1.2 pips | 1.3 pips |
| Prior TR: S3 / matched S2 / unmatched S2 | 86.3 / 86.0 / 105.5 | 91.8 / 90.7 / 98.7 |
| Paired mean expectancy: S3 / S2 | −13.77 / +10.33 | −12.36 / +8.72 |
| Difference (S3 − S2) | **−24.10 pips** | **−21.08 pips** |
| Permutation two-sided p | **0.030** | **0.142** |

> **The matching itself worked** — pairs are within ~1.3 pips of prior true range, and it
> mattered: unmatched S2 days are 12–19 pips more volatile the day before than S3 days, so
> the naive comparison would have been confounded in the direction PT-015 warned about.
>
> **And the result cannot be used.** n = 12 and n = 9. `SAMPLE INSUFFICIENT FOR INFERENCE —
> descriptive only.` The two arms return p = 0.030 and p = 0.142 on the same underlying data
> with a one-hour clock shift and three fewer pairs — which is what a p-value does at n = 9.
> **Quoting the 0.030 alone would be `E09` and `E24` at once, and it is not quoted anywhere
> in this document without the 0.142 beside it.**
>
> `PT-015` §5's row *"S3 clearly worse than S2 after matching → a ceiling exists in the
> data"* is the row this points at. **It is not claimed.** The honest statement is that the
> arm designed to decide the test was starved of data by the test's own assignment rule, and
> **the ceiling question `Q3` remains open exactly where `V04_INTERPRETATION.md` §3.3 left
> it.**

## 6. `D-031` — THE ARMS DIVERGE ON S1, AND THAT IS THE FINDING

| | Arm A (`UTC−5`) | Arm B (NY/DST) |
|---|---|---|
| S1 hit rate | 0.178 (63/353) | **0.224 (81/362)** |
| S1 expectancy | **−3.29 pips** | **−0.52 pips** |
| S1 percentile vs its own N1 (expectancy) | **0.2** — below the null's 5th percentile | **36.2** — mid-null |

**Same price path, same days, same trigger definition; the clock moved one hour.** On Arm A
the 10–24-pip location trigger is a *significantly worse-than-random* place to enter — the
null's 5–95% band is [−1.94, +1.58] pips and the observed value is −3.29. On Arm B the
identical construction is unremarkable.

`D-031` says divergence is a **finding, never a selection criterion.** What it finds here:
**the box's edges are timezone-sensitive enough to flip a significant negative into a null.**
The mechanism is visible in `BT_V04_0001` §7 — the median measured box is 31.0 pips on Arm A
and 26.7 on Arm B, so "10 pips beyond the edge" is not the same event on the two arms.

Reporting Arm B's S1 alone would hide a significant negative result; reporting Arm A's alone
would manufacture one. **Both are above.**

The arms **agree** on everything that matters to the question actually asked: S2 ≈ null, S1 ≈
S2, S3 empty of inferential content, S4 empty of data.

## 7. RULE APPLICATION VERSUS OUTCOME

`STUDY_PROTOCOL.md` §2 requires rule application be graded separately from trade result.

- **Rule application: 100% mechanical, no discretion exercised anywhere.** Stratum membership
  is a function of one close and the box; direction is a function of which edge; stop and
  target are the instructor's fixed numbers. There is no judgement to grade and none was
  applied.
- **Days excluded: only for coverage, and counted.** Three session days are removed by
  convention C-6 (§2a); every other session day with a box and a post-box window was offered
  to the classifier. The 38 (A) / 26 (B) days that never produced a close ≥ 10 pips beyond an
  edge simply have no trigger, and are counted, not dropped.
- **Losers retained:** 290 / 281 stop-outs are in the numbers above, and 63 / 66 positions
  still open at 17:00 are marked to market rather than discarded.
- **No parameter was adjusted at any point.** No stratum boundary, window, stop, target,
  seed or iteration count differs by one unit from the pre-registration.

## 8. WHAT THIS OBSERVATION DOES AND DOES NOT ESTABLISH

**Does:**
- **The ceiling question `Q3` is `NOT ANSWERED`** — and the reason is on the record: under
  PT-015's own pre-registered assignment rule, S3 reaches n = 12/9 and S4 is empty.
- **S2 (25–50 pips) is `INDISTINGUISHABLE FROM THE NULL`** on both arms, on both hit rate and
  expectancy, at n = 44/46.
- **S1 and S2 are not distinguishable from each other** (p = 0.205 / 0.240) — so the *floor*
  has no support at this sample either.
- **Random entry at these hours has no directional edge** (`D-029` secondary arm), and loses
  slowly against an 18/50 geometry whose break-even is 0.265.
- **On Arm A the S1 trigger is significantly worse than matched random entry** (percentile
  0.2); **on Arm B it is not** (36.2). A one-hour clock shift is the only difference.
- The volatility matching worked and the matched comparison is nonetheless unusable at n ≤ 12.

**Does not:**
- Establish what the instructor meant by *"25 to 50"* (`PT-015` §6 forbids it).
- Test the Market Maker Method entry rule, which needs `A-007`, `A-011` and `A-039`.
- Close `Q3`, `A-018`, `A-002`, `A-049`, `C-006` or any other record.
- Support **or** refute the existence of a ceiling. The pre-registered arm that would have
  decided it did not reach the sample it needed.
- License merging S3 and S4, re-cutting the strata, or re-issuing on a maximum-excursion rule
  without a new `PT` number (`D-027`).
- Generalise beyond GBP/USD, 2014-01-05 → 2015-12-31, and the V02 printed box.
