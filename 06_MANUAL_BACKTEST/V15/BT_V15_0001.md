## 0a. THE ONE-PARAGRAPH ANSWER

V15 says the dealer *"always"* ends the day **25–50 pips off the high or low**. On GBP/USD in
2014–2015 the day's close lands in that band **36.4%** of the time — and a **randomly chosen
minute of the same day**, scored against that same day's high and low, lands in it **37.6%** of
the time. **The real close does slightly WORSE than an arbitrary minute.** The stricter reading —
25–50 pips off the high **and** off the low, which is V10's wording — happens **9.4%** of the
time, below the 10% floor `PT-043` §6 fixed as the point at which an *"always"* is contradicted.
**The band is not empty. It is just not where the close goes, and nothing places it there.**

## 2. THE CONTROL IS THE FINDING

**`N1` holds the day's range fixed and varies only *where in the day* the close is read.** If the
dealer placed the close in a 25-pip-wide band off the nearer extreme, the real close would beat a
random minute. It does not — **in all four cells the delta is negative**, between −0.0117 and
−0.0225, and the observed rate sits inside the control's own 5th–95th percentile band in every
case (W-B/A: control `[0.3444, 0.4090]`, observed `0.3640`).

> **`O1`'s 36.4% is a fact about the distribution of GBP/USD's daily range, not about the close.**
> A day whose typical range is ~96 pips puts *any* moment somewhere, and 25–50 pips off the nearer
> extreme is simply a common place for a moment to be.

**This is precisely what the control was pre-registered to detect** (`PT-043` §5a), and it is why
a 36% hit-rate must not be reported as partial support for a rule. Without `N1`, *"the close is
25–50 pips off an extreme more than a third of the time"* reads as encouraging.

## 5. ⚠ THE ARM-B EXCLUSION ASYMMETRY — EXPLAINED, NOT A DEFECT

| Cell | included | excluded for incomplete sessions |
|---|---|---|
| W-B / A | 511 | **6** |
| W-B / B | 444 | **141** |
| W-A / A | 256 | **2** |
| W-A / B | 222 | **70** |

**Arm B drops ~24% of days and Arm A drops ~1%. That is the arm doing what it is defined to do,
not a bug.** Arm B shifts the clock by +1h through the DST intervals, so the corpus's week
boundary (Sunday 17:00 as served) no longer aligns with the session-day grid: the excluded dates
come in a **weekly pair pattern** — `2014-03-10 (92/96)`, `2014-03-15 (4/96)`, `2014-03-17
(92/96)`, `2014-03-22 (4/96)`, … — one day short four buckets at the week's start and one holding
only four at the week's end. `PT-043` §3a's completeness rule then removes both.

**Two things follow, and both are stated rather than buried:**

1. **The exclusions are structural and non-random**, so Arm B's `n` is not a random subsample of
   Arm A's. **This is a limitation of the Arm-B figure**, and it is why §1's headline is Arm A.
2. **It does not change the answer.** Arm B returns `O1 = 0.3604` against Arm A's `0.3640` — a
   0.36 pp difference on a 24%-different day set — and the same verdict in every cell.

**Every excluded date is named in `data/pt043_output.txt`.** The six Arm-A exclusions are
`2014-06-02` (the `D-036a` 22-hour hole, 8/96), `2014-12-24`, `2014-12-26`, `2015-01-01`,
`2015-08-21` and `2015-12-24` — holidays and the one known corpus defect, exactly as expected.

## 8. WHAT THIS DOES AND DOES NOT SHOW

### Does

1. **V15's `[00:31:27]` figure does not describe GBP/USD in 2014–2015**, on either reading, in
   either window, under either clock arm.
2. ⭐ **The stronger conclusion is `N1`'s, not the rate's:** even the 36% that *is* in band is not
   attributable to the close. **A random minute of the same day does marginally better.**
3. **The miss is in a specific direction** — the median nearer-distance is **21.4 pips**, just
   under the band's floor. The closes are **too close to the extreme**, not too far.
4. **Combined with `BT_V10_0001`**, two of this course's three quantitative pip claims about
   GBP/USD are now measured and neither holds: the **600–1000 pip week** (contradicted, 0/180) and
   now the **25–50 pip daily close** (not supported, and worse than random).

### Does not

1. **Nothing about other pairs.** The claim says *"depending on the pair"* and this is **GBP/USD
   only** — the pair the lesson's own chart shows (`V15_00-18-50_…png`, `GBPUSD,H4`). The
   qualifier is a live defence and is **not** rebutted here.
2. **Nothing about the underlying idea.** *"They want to end back in the range"* stated as a
   **relative** proposition — the close's percentile inside the day's range — is a different and
   untested question. ⚠ **It is deliberately NOT run here**, because inventing it after seeing
   this result is what `D-027` forbids. It is offered as a future `PT`, not as a rescue.
3. **Nothing about V15's actual lesson.** The ADR confluence rule is **untestable today**:
   `A-100` (no lookback, no range definition, no anchor) and `C-022` (*"creep"* vs *"not
   repaint"*) between them make a past ADR level's visibility undecidable. **Stated in `PT-043`
   §1b before the run, not offered afterwards.**
4. **Nothing about `A-084`, `A-085` or `A-086`.**
5. **Nothing about 2012.** `COMMON_PROTOCOL.md` §3's regime caveat stands: a 2012 claim is being
   measured on 2014–2015 data because that is the closest the corpus reaches.

## 9. LIMITATIONS — as declared in `PT-043` §9, plus two learned in the run

Declared in advance: one instrument; a 2014–2015 window against a 2012 claim; `D-034`'s
cross-vendor level caveat (which does not bite, since only differences are used); the
`2014-06-01` hole (excluded by completeness and named); `C-1` as a convention with `I-010` Q2 open;
and `n` in days, which are not independent.

**Learned in the run, and added:**

7. ⚠ **Arm B's completeness exclusions are structural, not random** (§5). Any future test using
   Arm B with a 96-bucket day rule inherits this and should expect ~24% attrition concentrated at
   week boundaries during DST intervals.
8. ⚠ **`N1` draws its pseudo-close from M1 bars weighted by their presence in the day.** On a
   complete day that is uniform over 1,440 minutes, which is what §5a specified; on a day with any
   thin patch it is uniform over *bars*, not over *minutes*. **Since only complete days are
   included, the two coincide here** — but the distinction is real and is recorded so a later
   reuse of this control does not inherit it silently.
