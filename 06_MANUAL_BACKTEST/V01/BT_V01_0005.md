# BT_V01_0005 — PT-032: the weekend gap, and the one mechanical rationale in V01

> ## ⚠ MANDATORY SCOPE STATEMENT — `PT-032` §6, verbatim
>
> **PT-032 measures the GBP/USD weekend gap.** It tests the **stated rationale** for the
> Friday-flat instruction, **not the instruction itself** — the instruction names no time, no
> session and no exception, so there is no rule with parameters to apply
> (`V01_INTERPRETATION.md` §7 `M5`). **No gap-fill trading rule may be derived from this test.**
>
> It **re-issues `PT-019`** onto `W-C′` under `D-035`; `PT-019` is retained, marked and never
> run, and no result here may be reported as `PT-019`'s result.
>
> **Every gap figure is bid-to-bid with no spread and is a LOWER BOUND on execution risk**
> (§0b). **Measure 4 is not a gap comparison on this corpus** and is labelled accordingly
> (§0c). **Price levels are not comparable with the V02–V06 FXCM homework** (`D-036a`); the
> **distances** reported here do travel. The **week open is 22:00 UTC**, not FXCM's 21:00 UTC.

## 0. PRE-REGISTRATION

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Claim under test | *"Don't carry trades through on Friday"* — the **only** V01 instruction whose rationale is **mechanical rather than pattern-based** (`V01_INTERPRETATION.md` §7 `M5`); *"You always get out on Friday, my friend, always"* V02 `[00:05:24]` |
| Pre-registration file | `PRE_REGISTERED/PT-032_weekend_gap_and_the_friday_flat_rule_reissue.md` — **not edited**. Re-issues `PT-019`, **never run** |
| Window | **`W-C′` — 2013-01-06 → 2016-06-30** |
| Gap definition | `open(first bar of the new trading week) − close(last bar of the previous week)`, **signed and absolute**. **Boundaries by timestamp lookup, never by bar count** |
| Decision point | **Friday close.** Everything after is outcome |
| Baselines | **Co-primary** Measure 4's Mon–Thu 17:00 changes (a **floor**, §0c) · **Co-primary, promoted** N3 week-anchor shift, run **first of all** |

### Both instants read from **M1**, because §3 forbids assuming either

> *"No boundary instant may be assumed … 15 of the corpus's 187 opens are late (17:01–17:10),
> and `D-036a` documents the week open but **not** the week close, so the Friday close is a
> run-time fact. **For this test in particular the whole measurement is those two instants.**"*

An **M15 aggregation would floor a 17:03 open into the 17:00 bucket** and silently erase the
lateness this test is required to measure, so **M1 is used throughout**. Realised:

```text
weeks whose open is LATE : 12 of 182   lags in minutes: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 10 (and 1321,
                                       which is the excluded 2014-06-01 hole)
realised week-close weekday : Fri = 179, Thu = 3
```

### Exclusions, applied and counted BY NAME

```text
2013-01-06  §3a   the gap INTO it needs the close of 2013-01-04 — inside the corpus,
                  OUTSIDE W-C'. Using it would silently widen the window (`D-027`).
2014-06-01  §3a″  THE FABRICATED GAP — EXCLUDED BY NAME. Also excluded: that week's
                  four Mon-Thu 17:00 instants from Measure 4 (§3a‴).
2016-06-26  §3a   the gap INTO it is fully determined by data before the boundary and
                  is RETAINED, even though the week itself is truncated.

WEEKEND GAPS USED : 180        (§3a‴ pre-registered: 180)  ✓
```

## 1. THE HARVEST CHECK, RUN FIRST OF ALL — §7 step 5

> *"**Run the shifted-boundary sanity control (N3) FIRST.** A non-zero result there means the
> harvest is wrong and no other number in the test can be trusted."* (§5 row 5: *"N3 returns
> non-zero 'gaps' at shifted boundaries → **Not a finding — a defect.**"*)

```text
median |shifted-boundary change| :  median 0.1 pips, 5-95% [0.1, 0.2]
N3 HARVEST CHECK                 :  PASS — near zero, the harvest is sound
```

**The runner raises and refuses to print any other figure if this exceeds 2 pips.** It did not.
Shifted week boundaries fall inside continuous trading and produce essentially nothing —
**0.1 pips against the real weekend's 8.90** — which is what separates a real discontinuity
from a harvest defect.

## 2. MEASURE 1 — THE DISTRIBUTION

> ⚠ **BID-TO-BID, NO SPREAD → A LOWER BOUND** (§0b). *"The Sunday reopen is precisely where a
> real spread is widest."* **This measurement error runs in favour of the instructor's
> rationale, which is exactly why §0b required it be declared before the result.**

| median | 90th | 99th | max |
|---|---|---|---|
| **8.90 pips** | **27.22** | 141.44 | **259.90** |

**§3a requires the top five be reported as an ordered list beside the 99th percentile**,
because *"the 99th percentile at n = 180 is an order statistic over roughly two observations"*:

| gap into | signed pips | realised closure |
|---|---|---|
| **2016-06-26** | **−259.90** | 48.0 h |
| 2014-09-07 | −158.50 | 48.0 h |
| 2016-02-21 | −136.90 | 48.0 h |
| 2016-06-19 | +113.00 | 48.0 h |
| 2013-02-24 | −84.00 | 48.0 h |

**The two largest are both June 2016** — the weekend into the EU referendum week (+113.0) and
the weekend immediately after the vote (−259.9). §0a.3 stated in advance that the referendum
weekend was *"very likely to be the largest in the sample, so that cannot later be presented as
a discovery."* **It is, and it is retained, not excluded.**

## 3. MEASURE 2 — THE RATIONALE, PRICED IN THE COURSE'S OWN UNITS

The thresholds are the instructor's own stop and target numbers (V04 `[00:04:43]`,
`[00:05:07]`) — **distances, so they travel across vendors**:

| threshold | share of weekends |
|---|---|
| > 10 pips | **0.467 (84/180)** [0.395, 0.539] |
| > 15 pips | **0.306 (55/180)** [0.243, 0.376] |
| **> 18 pips — the course's own stop** | **0.200 (36/180)** [0.148, 0.264] |
| > 50 pips — the course's own target | **0.044 (8/180)** [0.023, 0.085] ⚠ |

⚠ **The >50 subset is 8 — below 30.** §3a fixed this contingency in advance: the *share* is a
proportion over 180 and is reportable, but **any statement about what those weekends were like
carries `SAMPLE INSUFFICIENT FOR INFERENCE — descriptive only`.** The 10- and 15-pip buckets do
not have this problem, as §3a also predicted.

> ### **One weekend in five gaps straight through the stop the course teaches. `PT-032` §5 row 1 fires: *"The instructor's rationale holds on its own terms: an 18-pip stop cannot bound weekend risk, and the Friday-flat instruction is justified mechanically rather than doctrinally. The clearest supportable instruction in V01."***
>
> **And §0b means the true case is stronger than this.** A position carried through the weekend
> would be closed against a spread this corpus does not contain, at the moment of the week when
> spreads are widest. **20.0% is a floor on how often an 18-pip stop is jumped, not an
> estimate.**

---

## 4. MEASURE 4 — RELABELLED, AND IT IS A FLOOR

> ⚠ **§0c: on this corpus these are NOT gaps.** QA `C6` finds **three** intra-week gaps ≥ 30
> minutes in 3.5 years (4h43m total), so at 17:00 Mon–Thu consecutive bars simply abut. The
> control is **retained** — it holds the clock instant, the instrument and everything but
> calendar position fixed — and **relabelled** to what it measures: a **continuous-trading
> return across the 17:00 instant**. Comparing a discontinuous weekend jump against a
> continuous weekday return is **a floor, not an apples-to-apples null.**

```text
n = 714     (§3a‴ pre-registered ~720 with a FLOOR of ~714 — the realised figure is
             exactly that floor; 8 instants excluded for not being bracketed by a bar
             within 15 minutes on each side, counted)
median 0.30   90th 1.60   99th 4.10   max 19.90
share over 18 pips : 0.001 (1/714)
```

| | weekend | weekday 17:00 |
|---|---|---|
| median | **8.90 pips** | **0.30 pips** |
| **ratio** | **29.7×** | |
| share > 18 pips | **0.200 (36/180)** | **0.001 (1/714)** |

**The weekend jump is two orders of magnitude more likely to exceed the course's stop than the
same clock instant on a weekday.** As a floor, that is emphatic — and §0c is explicit that it
**does not** answer *"is the weekend jump bigger than other gaps?"*, because **this corpus
contains almost no other gaps to compare against.**

**QA `C6`'s three real intra-week gaps** are listed for completeness — `2015-01-01 13:33`,
`2015-08-21 13:16`, `2013-04-11 19:11` — and are **`DESCRIPTIVE ONLY` and never compared**:
three observations is far below any inferential floor.

## 6. MEASURE 5 — DIRECTIONAL, AND IT IS NULL

Is the gap's sign related to the prior Friday's direction? *"The dealer squares his books"*
predicts a relationship; **no relationship is the null.** §3a requires the four cell counts:

| | share | count |
|---|---|---|
| prior UP / gap UP | 0.540 [0.418, 0.657] | 34/63 |
| prior UP / gap DOWN | 0.460 [0.343, 0.582] | 29/63 |
| prior DOWN / gap UP | 0.496 [0.407, 0.585] | 58/117 |
| prior DOWN / gap DOWN | 0.504 [0.415, 0.593] | 59/117 |
| **gap CONTINUES the prior Friday's direction** | **0.517 [0.444, 0.589]** | **93/180** |

**Every interval contains 0.5. There is no directional relationship at this sample.** §5 row 4's
alternative — *"a genuinely new observation … an observation, not a rule, and not attributed to
the instructor"* — does **not** fire.

## 7. THE TWO CATEGORIES §3a′ AND §3a‴ REQUIRE BE KEPT SEPARATE

**The four mid-week holiday re-opens** — which `C7` miscounts as week opens, and which §3a′.1
calls *"precisely the class of fabricated observation this test exists to avoid producing"*.
**Reported separately by name, never merged into Measure 1:**

| re-open | move | closure |
|---|---|---|
| 2013-12-26 | 13.70 pips | 39.0 h |
| 2014-01-01 | 6.40 pips | 25.0 h |
| 2014-12-25 | 4.30 pips | 28.0 h |
| 2015-01-01 | 3.80 pips | 20.1 h |

**The two extended closures ≥ 72 h** — retained, and flagged with their realised duration,
because *"a 75-hour gap and a 48-hour gap are not the same event and a distribution that
silently mixes them is misleading"*:

| gap into | signed pips | realised closure |
|---|---|---|
| 2015-12-27 | **−7.10** | **75.1 h** |
| 2016-01-03 | **+0.40** | **72.0 h** |

*(ordinary weekends: median closure 48.0 h)*

> **§3a‴ expected these to be *"the strongest real evidence in the sample"* — a 75-hour closure
> being exactly what V01's rationale is about. They are the opposite: −7.10 and +0.40 pips,
> both far below the median.** The longest closures in the corpus produced two of its smallest
> gaps, because they are the Christmas and New Year weekends, when nothing happens. **That is a
> finding, and it is the reverse of the one the pre-registration anticipated** — which is why
> §3a‴ required they be flagged rather than averaged in.

## 8. `D-031` — THE ARMS

**Identical on both arms.** The gap is the difference between two prices at two physical
instants; relabelling the clock moves neither instant nor either price. §5 row 6 asks how much
of any divergence is the Arm-B week open moving to local 18:00 during DST — **the answer is
that it moves the label, not the instant, so there is no divergence at all.** Neither arm is
selected.

## 9. RULE APPLICATION VERSUS OUTCOME

The decision point is the **Friday close**, and everything after it is outcome. **N3 was run
first of all** (§7 step 5), with the runner set to abort the whole test on a non-zero result.
**Measure 4 was computed before the weekend distribution** (step 6). **Both boundary instants
were read from M1 timestamps and never assumed** (step 4), and the 12 late opens are reported.
**Every exclusion was applied and counted by name**, including the four Measure-4 instants
belonging to the holed week; the realised 180 and 714 match the pre-registered 180 and the
~714 floor exactly. **The four holiday re-open moves and the two extended closures are reported
separately** and never merged into Measure 1. **Every gap figure carries its lower-bound
label** (step 8). Neither `PT-032` nor `PT-019` was edited.

## 10. WHAT THIS OBSERVATION DOES AND DOES NOT ESTABLISH

**Does:**
- **The stated rationale for the Friday-flat instruction holds on its own terms.**
  **20.0% (36/180)** of weekends gap by more than the **18-pip stop the course itself teaches**;
  46.7% exceed 10 pips; 4.4% exceed 50. **An 18-pip stop cannot bound weekend risk.**
- **And the true case is stronger than measured** — §0b's missing spread makes every figure a
  **lower bound**, at the moment of the week when spreads are widest.
- **The weekend is categorically unlike the same clock instant on a weekday**: median 8.90 vs
  0.30 pips (**29.7×**), and over-18-pip moves at 0.200 vs **0.001**. As a **floor**.
- **Confirms the harvest** — N3 at shifted boundaries returns a median of **0.1 pips**, so the
  measured discontinuity is real and not an artifact.
- **Finds no directional relationship** (0.517, 93/180, interval containing 0.5) — the *"squares
  his books"* reading is not supported.
- **Reverses one of the pre-registration's own expectations**, on the record: the two ≥ 72 h
  extended closures were expected to be the strongest evidence and are two of the smallest gaps
  in the sample (−7.10 and +0.40 pips), because they are Christmas and New Year.

**Does not:**
- **Test the instruction itself** — it names no time, no session and no exception, so there is
  no rule with parameters to apply (`M5`).
- **Derive any gap-fill rule.** 73.9% fill within 4 hours; **no strategy is stated or implied**,
  and §6 forbids one. A gap that fills in 37 minutes still gapped *through* the stop.
- Answer *"is the weekend jump bigger than other gaps?"* — **this corpus contains almost no
  other gaps** (§0c), and its three real intra-week gaps are descriptive only.
- Permit comparison of these levels with the V02–V06 FXCM homework; only the **distances**
  travel.
- Generalise beyond GBP/USD and 2013-01-06 → 2016-06-30. **The October 2016 flash crash is in
  HOLDOUT and was not opened**, which §0a.2 notes limits what the **tail** of this distribution
  can be said to represent.
