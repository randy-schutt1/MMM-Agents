# BT_V01_0004 — PT-028: on which weekday does the week make its high and its low?

> ## ⚠ MANDATORY SCOPE STATEMENT — `PT-028` §6, verbatim
>
> **PT-028 measures when GBP/USD weekly extremes print.** It is **not** a test of the anchor
> point (`A-001`), the midweek reversal (`A-012`), the peak formation (`A-010`) or the M/W
> (`A-011`) — none of which is defined in V01–V06 — and it adopts **no** day count, so it takes
> no position on `C-001`. A clustering result would be *consistent with* the taught cycle; it
> would not identify an anchor on any single week, which is what a trader would actually need.
>
> It **re-issues `PT-010`** onto `W-C′` under `D-035`; `PT-010` is retained, marked and never
> run, and no result here may be reported as `PT-010`'s result.
>
> **The Sunday and Friday cells are below n = 30 and are descriptive only** (§3d). **The 6 × 6
> joint table is descriptive only and carries no χ².** The **week open is 22:00 UTC**, not
> FXCM's 21:00 UTC.

## 2. THE EXPOSURE-WEIGHTED NULL, COMPUTED BEFORE ANY EXTREME WAS LOCATED

`PT-028` §3a: *"A per-weekday-uniform null (1/6 each) is wrong and would manufacture a
result."* Weights are computed **per week and per arm from realised bars**, so a closed
Wednesday contributes zero Wednesday exposure.

**Arm A** (Arm B in brackets where it differs):

| | Sun | Mon | Tue | Wed | Thu | Fri |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| mean hours/week | **6.92** [6.32] | 23.73 | 23.67 | 23.54 | 23.54 | **16.72** [17.37] |
| share | 5.9% | 20.1% | 20.0% | 19.9% | 19.9% | 14.2% |
| **expected count** | **10.5** [9.5] | 36.0 | 35.8 | 35.6 | 35.7 | **25.4** [26.3] |

> **`PT-028` §3d predicted these two cells before the run** — *"Sunday 10.5, Friday 25.5,
> both below 30 — descriptive only"* — and the realised figures are **10.5 and 25.4**, i.e.
> the prediction was accurate to a tenth. §3a's claim that Arm B resizes them is also
> confirmed: **Sunday 6.32 h and Friday 17.37 h under DST**, against Arm A's 6.92 and 16.72.
>
> **The 2014-06-01 data hole was excluded BEFORE these weights were computed**, per §3c —
> including it would have recorded 22 missing hours as *genuine zero exposure*, encoding a
> corpus defect as a market fact in the thinnest cell.

## 3. RESULT — MEASURE 1, AND THE FOCAL PREDICTION FAILS

**Arm A**, n = 180 weeks per marginal:

| | Sun | Mon | Tue | Wed | Thu | **Fri** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| expected | 10.5 | 36.0 | 35.8 | 35.6 | 35.7 | 25.4 |
| **HIGHS** | 18 | 35 | **22** | **23** | 37 | **45** |
| **LOWS** | 14 | 30 | 30 | **28** | **18** | **60** |
| high ÷ exp | **1.72** | 0.97 | **0.61** | **0.65** | 1.04 | **1.77** |
| low ÷ exp | **1.33** | 0.83 | 0.84 | 0.79 | **0.50** | **2.37** |

**Arm B**: highs `18 / 34 / 22 / 24 / 35 / 47`, lows `14 / 30 / 30 / 28 / 18 / 60`.
**Both modes Friday on both arms.**

| | χ² (six-cell marginal) | Monte-Carlo p |
|---|---|---|
| HIGHS, Arm A | 30.49 | **< 0.001** |
| LOWS, Arm A | 60.83 | **< 0.001** |
| HIGHS, Arm B | 32.98 | **< 0.001** |
| LOWS, Arm B | 57.47 | **< 0.001** |

> ### **The distribution is emphatically non-uniform — and the mode of BOTH the high and the low is FRIDAY, on both `D-031` arms.**
>
> **`PT-028`'s pre-registered focal prediction — *"the mode of both marginal distributions
> falls on Tuesday or Wednesday"* — FAILS.**
>
> Tuesday and Wednesday are not merely not-modal: they are **the two most depleted cells in
> the high distribution** (0.61 and 0.65 of expectation). Thursday is the most depleted for
> lows (0.50). **The days the course names as the turning point are the days the week's
> extremes least often print.**
>
> ⚠ **The Friday mode carries the §3d label**, as `PT-028` §5 row 2 requires in advance:
> *"If the mode is Friday or Sunday, it lands in a cell flagged §3d-marginal and must be
> reported with that label attached — a marginal mode is still a finding, and hiding the label
> would be `E24`."* Friday's **expected** count is 25.4, below the n = 30 floor, so
> **`SAMPLE INSUFFICIENT FOR INFERENCE — descriptive only` attaches to any rate quoted from
> it.** Its *observed* counts (45 highs, 60 lows) are not themselves small, and the χ² above
> is computed on the marginal as a whole, which §3d permits.

### The shape: the week's extremes sit at the week's EDGES

Reading the ratio rows across, both marginals are **U-shaped**: elevated on Sunday
(1.72 / 1.33) and Friday (1.77 / 2.37), depleted through Tuesday–Thursday. **Measure 2
confirms it independently**, without depending on the day boundary at all:

```text
hour-of-week of the HIGH, 4-hour bins (30 bins, 6.0 per bin) — Arm A, COUNTS ONLY:
 12  7 14  7  8  3  0  3  8  6  5  1  0  1  3  5  5  6  1  4  8  8 11  3  4  3  7 13 15  9
 |__ week open                                                              week close __|
busiest: 112-116h (15), 8-12h (14), 108-112h (13), 0-4h (12)
```

**The four busiest bins are the two at the very start of the week and the two at the very
end.** The emptiest bins (0, 1, 0 counts) sit at 24–28 h, 48–52 h and 72–76 h — the middle.

> **This is the arcsine/endpoint concentration `BT_V01_0001` §2 measured at day scale,
> reappearing at week scale.** A random-walk path puts its extremes near the ends of any
> window. **`PT-028` §3d forbids per-bin inference here** (6.0 per bin), so the histogram is
> reported as counts and the shape is read from it qualitatively, not tested bin by bin.

## 4. THE TWO SHIFT CONTROLS — AND THEY ARE WHAT STOPS THIS BEING OVERSOLD

| Baseline | Modal weekday share of highs | Observed 0.333 sits at |
|---|---|---|
| **N3 — week-anchor shift** | median **0.3483**, 5–95% [0.2921, 0.4607] | **percentile 0.0** |
| **Randomly re-blocked 5-day spans** | median **0.2416**, 5–95% [0.1966, 0.2941] | percentile 70.1 |

> ### **The edge concentration is real, but it is NOT a property of the calendar week.**
>
> Under **N3**, a week anchored anywhere produces a modal share of **0.3483** — *higher* than
> the real calendar week's 0.3333. `PT-028` §4 wrote the control for exactly this: *"If
> extremes cluster mid-week under **any** week anchor, the finding is about the middle of a
> 5-day span, not about Tuesday."* The same logic applies to the edges, and it applies here:
> **a sham week concentrates its extremes at its own edges just as hard.**
>
> Against the **re-blocked 5-day spans** the observed share is higher (percentile 70.1) but
> does not clear the 95th.
>
> **So two things are true at once and both belong in the summary:** the weekday distribution
> of GBP/USD weekly extremes is **strongly non-uniform and its shape contradicts the taught
> one**, and **the non-uniformity is not evidence that the calendar week is a special unit.**

## 5. MEASURE 3 — THE JOINT TABLE AND THE SIGN

**The 6 × 6 joint table is RAW COUNTS, DESCRIPTIVE ONLY, and carries NO χ² and NO p-value**
(§3d point 3: 36 cells over 180 weeks averages **5.0 per cell**, and the Sunday row/column
average ~1). Arm A:

```text
     low ->   Sun   Mon   Tue   Wed   Thu   Fri
  hi Sun       0     0     1     5     4     8
  hi Mon       0     0     1     7     4    23
  hi Tue       1     1     0     2     4    14
  hi Wed       2     7     7     1     1     5
  hi Thu       6     8     6     8     0     9
  hi Fri       5    14    15     5     5     1
```

**The inferentially usable part of Measure 3** — the sign of `high_day − low_day`, a single
ordinal summary at n = 180:

| | Arm A | Arm B |
|---|---|---|
| high **after** low (+) | **0.500 (90/180)** [0.428, 0.572] | 0.500 (90/180) |
| high **before** low (−) | **0.489 (88/180)** [0.417, 0.561] | 0.489 (88/180) |
| same day (0) | 2 | 2 |

> ### **The week has no direction of travel. 90 against 88 is as close to a coin as 180 observations can get.**
>
> `PT-028` §3 calls Measure 3 *"the one that distinguishes a real cycle from a boundary
> artifact: a week that sets its low early and its high late is a trend week, not a cycle
> week."* **The sample contains equal numbers of each**, which is what an absence of cyclical
> structure looks like.

## 6. `D-031` — THE ARMS

**The two arms agree on every conclusion.** Both give Friday as the mode of both marginals,
both reject uniformity at p < 0.001, both give the identical sign split (90/88/2), and their
weekday counts differ by at most 2 in any cell. The **only** systematic difference is the one
§3a predicted: Arm B's DST weeks resize Sunday to 6.32 h and Friday to 17.37 h, moving the
expected counts to 9.5 and 26.3. **That is exposure arithmetic, not market behaviour**, and
§5 row 4 asks that it be stated as such. **Neither arm is selected.**

## 8. WHAT THIS OBSERVATION DOES AND DOES NOT ESTABLISH

**Does:**
- **The pre-registered focal prediction fails.** The mode of both the weekly high and the
  weekly low is **Friday** on both `D-031` arms, not Tuesday or Wednesday.
- **The taught calendar shape is contradicted, not merely unsupported.** Tuesday and Wednesday
  are the **two most depleted** cells for highs (0.61 and 0.65 of exposure-weighted
  expectation); Thursday is the most depleted for lows (0.50). **The week's extremes print at
  the week's edges.**
- The weekday distribution **is** strongly non-uniform — χ² p < 0.001 on both marginals and
  both arms, against an exposure-weighted null.
- **Measure 2 confirms the U-shape independently of the day boundary** — the four busiest of
  30 hour-of-week bins are the two at the week's open and the two at its close.
- **The week has no direction of travel**: high-after-low 90, high-before-low 88, at n = 180.
- **The non-uniformity is NOT evidence for the calendar week as a unit.** Under N3 a week
  anchored anywhere concentrates its extremes at its own edges at least as hard (observed
  percentile **0.0**).
- Confirms §3d's advance arithmetic to a tenth: expected Sunday **10.5**, Friday **25.4**.

**Does not:**
- Test the anchor point (`A-001`), the midweek reversal (`A-012`), the peak formation
  (`A-010`) or the M/W (`A-011`), and adopts **no** day count — `C-001` is untouched.
- Support any per-bin or per-joint-cell claim — **no χ² was computed on the joint table** and
  **no per-bin inference** was drawn from Measure 2.
- Establish that the Friday mode is tradeable, or identify an anchor on any single week.
- Establish that the week's edges are special **because they are the week's edges** — the
  arcsine concentration of a random-walk path predicts edge-loading for any window, and N3
  shows the calendar anchor adds nothing.
- Generalise beyond GBP/USD and 2013-01-06 → 2016-06-30. **The 2017 regime and the October
  2016 flash crash are in HOLDOUT and were not opened.**
