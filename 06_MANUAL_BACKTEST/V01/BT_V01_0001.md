# BT_V01_0001 — PT-002 (W-A arm): do DAILY extremes cluster at the six printed boundaries?

> ## ⚠ MANDATORY SCOPE STATEMENT — `PT-002` §6, verbatim
>
> PT-002 tests whether GBP/USD turning points cluster at six printed clock boundaries. It
> is **not** a test of the Market Maker Method's trap move, which is `A-002` and remains
> undefined as a pattern. A favourable result supports the *premise* that timing carries
> information. It says nothing about whether a trap move can be recognised at the hard
> right edge, and nothing about any entry.

> ## ⚠ WHICH ARM THIS IS — read before quoting any number
>
> `PT-002` declares **two** windows. This observation runs the **W-A DAILY arm only**.
>
> Its **W-C weekly arm is NON-CONFORMING under `D-035`** (it straddles the pinned
> DEVELOPMENT/HOLDOUT boundary by 546 days), was **never run**, and is re-issued as
> **`PT-025`** on `W-C′`. **No number here may be reported as PT-002's result** without
> that qualification, and none may be reported as PT-025's.

## 2. THE TWO BASELINES, BUILT FIRST — AND THEY DISAGREE

`COMMON_PROTOCOL.md` §9.1 requires the baselines be run before the rule arm's
aggregate is looked at. They were, and they did not agree:

| Band | Analytic expectation | N2 median | Gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| ±15 min | 0.1667 | 0.2328 | **+40%** |
| **±30 min** | **0.2500** | **0.3228** | **+29%** |
| ±60 min | 0.4167 | 0.4951 | **+19%** |

`PT-002` §4 row 3 is explicit that this must be investigated rather than reported:
*"A sanity check on N2; **a large disagreement between them is a bug, not a finding.**"*

**It was investigated, and it is not a bug.** The diagnostic is the position of the
extreme **inside the shifted session day**, measured in the N2 frame:

| | Arm A | Arm B | Uniform |
|---|---|---|---|
| first 10% of the day | **0.1824** | 0.1826 | 0.1042 |
| last 10% of the day | **0.1616** | 0.1613 | 0.1042 |
| middle 20% | **0.1673** | 0.1675 | 0.2083 |

> ### The distribution is **U-shaped** — the arcsine law for the argmax of a random-walk path.
>
> A day's extreme sits near that day's **edges** far more often than uniform time
> predicts, **wherever the edges are placed**. So any boundary that is also a day
> boundary collects extremes for reasons that have nothing to do with the clock being
> the *right* clock.
>
> **N2 absorbs this by construction, because it re-derives the session days under each
> shifted clock. The analytic uniform-time expectation cannot represent it at all.**
> The disagreement is a **structural property of the price path**, not a harvest defect.
> N2 remains the pre-registered primary and governs the verdict; the analytic figure is
> retained and reported rather than discarded.

**This is the single most consequential thing in this observation**, because it is the
entire difference between the headline reading and the null one — see §3.

## 3. RESULT — THE OBSERVED SHARE

**Arm A** (n = 256 days, 512 extremes):

| Band | Observed | Analytic | N2 median | **N2 percentile** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ±15 min | 0.230 (118/512) [0.196, 0.269] | 0.1667 | 0.2328 | **46.9** |
| **±30 min** | **0.338 (173/512) [0.298, 0.380]** | **0.2500** | **0.3228** | **60.6** |
| ±60 min | 0.539 (276/512) [0.496, 0.582] | 0.4167 | 0.4951 | **74.3** |

**Arm B** (n = 222 days, 444 extremes):

| Band | Observed | Analytic | N2 median | **N2 percentile** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ±15 min | 0.227 (101/444) [0.191, 0.269] | 0.1667 | 0.2275 | **49.7** |
| **±30 min** | **0.302 (134/444) [0.261, 0.346]** | **0.2500** | **0.3220** | **39.1** |
| ±60 min | 0.466 (207/444) [0.420, 0.513] | 0.4167 | 0.4951 | **37.9** |

> ### **Against the analytic expectation the result looks positive: 0.338 versus 0.250 is a 35% excess. Against N2 it vanishes — percentile 60.6, squarely inside the null distribution.**
>
> **The excess is the arcsine artifact of §2, not the clock.** Reporting the 35% figure
> as the headline would have been a confirmation manufactured by using the weaker of two
> pre-registered baselines, and `PT-002` §4 names N2 the primary precisely because it is
> the one that isolates *"does the clock carry the information?"*
>
> **Arm B does not merely fail to confirm — it sits BELOW the N2 median** at the
> headline band (39.1) and at ±60 (37.9).
>
> `PT-002` §5 row 1 fires: **"The six boundaries carry no detectable timing information
> on GBP/USD at this sample. A foundational null — report prominently, do not bury."**

**Highs and lows separately**, since they are separate claims — Arm A: highs 0.340
(87/256), lows 0.336 (86/256), indistinguishable from each other. Arm B: highs 0.347
(77/222), lows **0.257 (57/222)** — a divergence that does not appear on Arm A and that
this sample cannot explain.

## 4. PER-BOUNDARY BREAKDOWN — `PT-002` §5 row 2

The pre-registration calls this *"the most likely real outcome and the most useful"*.
Expected share is stated beside observed, as §3a requires. **Categories overlap** — 17:00
is simultaneously a day close, a day open and a New York session close — so these rows
do not sum to the headline.

**Arm A**, ±30 min:

| Boundary | Observed | Expected | Ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| **week_open** | 9/512 = 0.0176 | 0.0063 | **2.77** ⚠ |
| day_open | 29/512 = 0.0566 | 0.0481 | 1.18 ⚠ |
| session_open | 92/512 = 0.1797 | 0.1562 | 1.15 |
| **session_close** | **116/512 = 0.2266** | 0.1499 | **1.51** |
| day_close | 28/512 = 0.0547 | 0.0457 | 1.20 ⚠ |
| **week_close** | 8/512 = 0.0156 | 0.0040 | **3.92** ⚠ |

**Arm B**, ±30 min: week_open 5/444 (ratio 4.44 ⚠), day_open 24/444 (1.14 ⚠),
session_open 85/444 (1.23), session_close 90/444 (1.32), day_close 25/444 (1.14 ⚠),
week_close 6/444 (2.94 ⚠).

> ⚠ = **cell count below n = 30: `SAMPLE INSUFFICIENT FOR INFERENCE — descriptive only`**
> (`COMMON_PROTOCOL.md` §9.4). **Four of the six cells are below the floor on both arms**,
> including both week boundaries — which is where the largest ratios sit. **The two
> largest ratios in the table are the two least reliable numbers in it**, and that is a
> property of the design, not a discovery: a daily-extreme test gets one week open per
> five session days, so the weekly cells can never be well-sampled from W-A. **The weekly
> boundaries are properly tested only by `PT-025`, on weekly extremes, where they are the
> unit of analysis.**

The one cell that is both above the floor and materially elevated on both arms is
**session_close** (1.51 / 1.32) — driven by the 17:00 and 09:00 instants. It is reported
as an observation. **It does not survive as a headline**, because the aggregate it
belongs to does not clear N2.

## 7. WHAT THIS OBSERVATION DOES AND DOES NOT ESTABLISH

**Does:**
- **The six printed boundaries carry no detectable timing information for GBP/USD daily
  extremes in 2015**, on either `D-031` arm, at the pre-registered ±30 min band
  (N2 percentile 60.6 / 39.1) or at either sensitivity band.
- **Establishes why a naive version of this test would have confirmed the claim.**
  Against the analytic uniform expectation the observation reads as a 35% excess; the
  excess is the **arcsine concentration of a random walk's extremes at any day
  boundary**, measured directly in §2 (first/last 10% of the day at 0.18/0.16 against a
  uniform 0.10). **The clustering is real; the clock is not what causes it.**
- Records that **four of six per-boundary cells are below n = 30 on both arms**,
  including both week boundaries, and that the two largest ratios in the table sit in
  those cells.

**Does not:**
- Test the trap move (`A-002`), which is undefined as a pattern, or any entry.
- Say anything about **weekly** extremes. That is `PT-025` on `W-C′`, and the week
  boundaries are under-sampled here by construction (§4).
- Close `A-002`, `A-019`, `I-010`, or resolve which `D-031` arm is correct.
- Generalise beyond GBP/USD and 2015-01-04 → 2015-12-31.
- Support the session_close elevation (§4) as a finding — its aggregate does not clear
  the primary null.
