## 2. A DEFINITION THE PRE-REGISTRATION DOES NOT SUPPLY, STATED RATHER THAN ASSUMED

`PT-027` §4 names the third baseline *"second and later breaches of the same block in the same
week"* but **does not define what counts as a breach event.** The first implementation counted
**every 15m close that sat outside the block**, which produced **66,443 "later breaches" across
180 weeks — 369 per week.** That is a count of bars spent outside the block, not a count of
breaches, and it would have made the third baseline a different quantity from the headline
trigger it exists to contrast with.

**The definition adopted, stated here and applied identically to the first breach and to every
later one:** a breach occurs at a close whose side (above / inside / below the block) **differs
from the previous close's side** and is not "inside". Re-crossing an edge after returning inside
is a new breach; running from above to below without closing inside is also a new breach.

```text
later breaches under the corrected definition: 1,586 across 171 weeks  (~9 per week)
```

**The headline trigger is unaffected by this choice** — the first close beyond an edge is a
crossing under either reading — so **only the third baseline changed**, and it changed from
nonsense to a usable control.

## 3. RESULT — OUTCOME 3, THE PROHIBITION PRICED

> ⚠ **BID-ONLY, NO SPREAD, NO SLIPPAGE → EVERY FIGURE BELOW IS AN UPPER BOUND** (`PT-027`
> §0c). A real fill would face a spread that is not in this corpus. **The measurement error
> runs in favour of the trade, so a result showing the trade LOSES is stronger than it looks,
> not weaker.** This direction was declared in advance, which is what makes the result
> readable.

**Arm A and Arm B are identical on the headline arm** — the 8-hour block runs from one physical
instant and a timezone relabelling cannot move a physical span (the same structural fact
`BT_V03_0003` §2 establishes).

|---|---|---|---|
| triggers | 180 / 180 weeks | 180 / 180 | pool 79,792 eligible entries |
| **target hit (50 pips)** | **0.189 (34/180)** [0.138, 0.252] | 0.189 (34/180) | median **0.2545** [0.2024, 0.3059] |

*(✓ = clears the pre-registered null — below the 5th percentile of N1, i.e. significantly
**worse** than a matched random entry, which is the direction the prohibition predicts.)*

> ### **Taking the first move of the week loses 5.16 pips per trade, and only 0.9% of matched random-entry draws did worse. The instructor's prohibition is doing real work.**
>
> A matched random entry in the same weeks, the same eligible hours, the same direction mix and
> the same 18/50 stop and target is roughly **break-even (−0.20 pips)**. The first breach is
> **−5.16**. The gap is not the trade being unprofitable in general — it is *this* entry being
> **specifically bad**.
>
> **And §0c means the true case is at least this strong**, because the spread the corpus does
> not contain would make the realised loss larger, not smaller.

### The third baseline — the course's own contrast, and it points the same way

| | first breach | **second and later breaches** |
|---|---|---|
| n | 180 (180 weeks) | **1,586 (171 weeks)** |
| target hit | 0.189 (34/180) | **0.277 (424/1,533)** [0.255, 0.300] |
| expectancy | **−5.16 pips** | **+1.17 pips** |

> **V01 forbids the *first* move; V02–V03 endorse the *return*. Holding week, instrument,
> block and geometry fixed while varying only the ordinal, the course's own contrast comes out
> in the course's own direction** — the first breach loses, later breaches of the same block do
> not. **171 weeks produce a later breach**, so §3a's contingency (*"if the number of weeks
> producing a second breach falls below 30, that control is descriptive only"*) does **not**
> fire and the control is inferentially usable.

## 7. WHAT THIS OBSERVATION DOES AND DOES NOT ESTABLISH

**Does:**
- **The prohibited trade loses, and loses distinctively.** Expectancy **−5.16 pips** against a
  matched random entry's −0.20; **N1 percentile 0.9** on expectancy and **2.5** on target-hit
  rate, on both `D-031` arms and both block arms. **`PT-027` §5 row 1 fires: the prohibition
  is doing real work.** Necessary support for V01's central instruction.
- **The course's own contrast holds in the course's own direction**: second-and-later breaches
  of the same block return **+1.17 pips** and hit target **0.277**, against the first breach's
  −5.16 and 0.189, over 1,586 events in 171 weeks.
- **The reversal is real and strong** — 0.794 of weeks trade back through the *opposite* edge,
  median 8.0 hours later.
- **The "trapped for the entire week" framing is NOT supported.** The week's extreme lands on
  the breach side in 0.550 of weeks, with a 95% interval **containing 0.50**, and forms a median
  **81 hours** after the trigger.
- Because §0c's missing spread runs **in favour** of the trade, **the true case for the
  prohibition is at least this strong.**

**Does not:**
- Close `A-006` — what "the box" refers to in V01 `[00:43:07]` **remains OPEN**. This test
  measures one drawable operationalisation and says so.
- Identify a "false move" or a "trap move" (`A-002`). The trigger is a range breach and is
  reported as one.
- **Supply a tradeable rule.** A losing entry is not an edge in the opposite direction: the
  reverse trade is untested here, would face the same absent spread, and nothing in this test
  identifies the entry in real time.
- Support the 12-hour block — **not carried here**, and §0b records that omission as a choice.
- Generalise beyond GBP/USD and 2013-01-06 → 2016-06-30. **The 2017 regime and the October 2016
  flash crash are in HOLDOUT and were not opened.**
