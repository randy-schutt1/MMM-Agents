# BT_V02_0002 — PT-021: DNC and the straightaway test

> ## ⚠ MANDATORY SCOPE STATEMENT — `PT-021` §6, verbatim
>
> PT-021 tests whether a prior opposite-side breach of the Asian range changes what follows.
> It uses that breach as a **geometric event** and does **not** identify a stop hunt:
> `A-002` is undefined, `A-049` records that the course has never stated a discriminator,
> and `C-006` records that the two guest presenters who tried gave incompatible accounts —
> **both of which are excluded from doctrine by `D-025` and neither of which is used here.**
> This is not a test of the Market Maker Method entry rule, which additionally requires an
> M/W second leg (`A-007`, `A-011`) and TDI (`A-039`).
>
> *(`D-025` was reversed by `D-033` on 2026-08-13, after this file was written. The
> reversal does not affect this test: no guest account of the stop hunt is used here under
> either decision, because `A-002`/`A-049` remain undefined and `D-030` is untouched.)*

### 1a. A terminology conflict between two pre-registered files, declared

`PT-015` §3 calls *"breach high → short"* the direction **"away from the box"**. `PT-021` §3
calls the identical geometry the **"counter-trade (back into the range)"**. The **words**
disagree between two pre-registered files; the **geometry** is unambiguous and identical in
both, and it is the geometry that is implemented: **breach high → short, breach low → long.**

**Neither file is edited** (`INDEX.md` §5 rule 1). The conflict is recorded because a later
session comparing `BT_V04_0002` with this document will otherwise think the two tests took
opposite sides of the same trade. They did not.

## 4. MEASURE 2 — THE DNC TEST. **THE PROHIBITION IS NOT SUPPORTED**

The prohibited counter-trade, priced in **both** classes. `PT-021` §3: *"the only way to
test a prohibition honestly is to price the prohibited trade in both classes."*

**The prohibition predicts: this LOSES in class SA, and is ACCEPTABLE in class SW.**

**Arm A (`UTC−5`)**

| | SA — *"do not counter"* | SW — *"that's a trade"* |
|---|---|---|
| n | 293 | 100 |
| target / stop / open at 17:00 | 53 / 186 / 54 | 14 / 50 / 36 |
| **Hit rate (of resolved)** | **0.222 (53/239)** [0.174, 0.279] | **0.219 (14/64)** [0.135, 0.334] |
| **Expectancy** | **−0.45 pips** [−3.48, +2.66] | **+2.15 pips** [−2.53, +7.00] |
| Outcome SD / IQR | 26.6 / 29.5 | 24.8 / 35.4 |
| Percentile vs its own N1 (hit / exp) | 80.1 / 43.7 | 67.6 / 85.1 |

**Arm B (`America/New_York`)**

| | SA | SW |
|---|---|---|
| n | 263 | 142 |
| target / stop / open at 17:00 | 43 / 168 / 52 | 21 / 81 / 40 |
| **Hit rate (of resolved)** | **0.204 (43/211)** [0.155, 0.263] | **0.206 (21/102)** [0.139, 0.294] |
| **Expectancy** | **−0.98 pips** [−3.97, +1.96] | **+0.10 pips** [−3.97, +4.21] |
| Outcome SD / IQR | 26.1 / 29.4 | 25.0 / 32.6 |
| Percentile vs its own N1 (hit / exp) | 51.1 / 25.4 | 53.9 / 53.8 |

**The primary baseline — the course's own contrast:**

| | Arm A | Arm B |
|---|---|---|
| Mean expectancy SA − SW | **−2.60 pips** | **−1.08 pips** |
| Permutation two-sided p | **0.404** | **0.679** |
| Hit rate SA vs SW | 0.222 vs 0.219 | 0.204 vs 0.206 |

> ### **The hit rates are indistinguishable — 0.222 vs 0.219 and 0.204 vs 0.206.**
>
> `PT-021` §5's row *"Both classes behave alike → The binary distinguishes nothing at this
> sample. **Report prominently** — it is the most confidently graded conditional in V02 and
> its failure would be a significant finding about the lesson"* — **is the row that fires.**
>
> The expectancy point estimates lean the way the prohibition predicts (SA negative, SW
> positive on both arms), but the permutation tests return **p = 0.404 and p = 0.679**, no
> class clears its own N1's 95th percentile (best is 85.1), and every interval overlaps every
> other interval.
>
> **The prohibited trade is a slightly-losing trade in class SA and a roughly-flat trade in
> class SW, and the sample cannot tell them apart.** Both are inside the spread this test
> declines to model. Neither class produced a hit rate whose interval excludes the 0.265
> break-even of an 18/50 geometry — **the counter-trade is not worth taking in either class**,
> which is a different and weaker statement than the one V02 makes.

## 5. THE THIRD BASELINE — AND THE CONFOUND IT WAS BUILT FOR IS LARGELY ABSENT

`PT-021` §5 calls the volatility confound *"the likeliest confound in the whole batch"*.
**It is measured, and on Arm B it is not there.**

| | Arm A: SA / SW | Arm B: SA / SW |
|---|---|---|
| Median **prior** true range | 99.9 / 91.8 pips | 97.1 / 96.4 pips |
| Median box range | 33.1 / 26.2 pips | 29.7 / 24.2 pips |
| Expectancy ÷ prior TR | −0.0116 / +0.0430 | −0.0241 / −0.0009 |
| Prior-TR difference SA − SW | +8.1 pips, **p = 0.217** | +0.7 pips, **p = 0.931** |
| **Normalised expectancy SA − SW** | **−0.0546 of prior TR, p = 0.142** | **−0.0232 of prior TR, p = 0.462** |

**The two classes are not drawn from materially different volatility regimes** (p = 0.217 /
0.931 on prior true range). So the confound the third arm exists to catch **did not arise**,
and normalising does not rescue the comparison: the normalised difference is null on both
arms (p = 0.142 / 0.462).

`PT-021` §5's row *"The difference disappears under volatility normalisation → the classes
differ because two-sided days are wider days"* **does not fire either** — there was no
difference to disappear.

**One real asymmetry does survive:** SW days have a **visibly smaller box** (26.2 vs 33.1
pips on Arm A; 24.2 vs 29.7 on Arm B). A day whose Asian range is narrow is mechanically
easier to breach on both sides, which is what class SW *is*. That is a property of the
classifier, not a finding about dealers, and it is recorded so nobody later reads it as one.

## 6. `D-031` — THE ARMS AGREE ON THE VERDICT AND DISAGREE ON THE SPLIT

| | Arm A | Arm B |
|---|---|---|
| SA / SW day counts | 294 / 100 | 263 / **143** |
| SA vs SW hit rate | 0.222 vs 0.219 | 0.204 vs 0.206 |
| SA − SW expectancy, p | −2.60, p = 0.404 | −1.08, p = 0.679 |
| Continuation to 17:00, p | +10.7, **p = 0.022** | +4.8, p = 0.291 |

**The verdict is identical on both arms: no separation on the DNC test.** The arms differ in
two places, and both are findings rather than defects:

1. **Class SW grows 43% under a one-hour clock shift** (100 → 143 days). The event the
   instructor points at when he says *"the dealer made a stop hunt"* is, operationally,
   partly an artifact of where the box is drawn.
2. **The only p < 0.05 anywhere in this test appears on one arm and not the other.** That is
   precisely the pattern `D-031` was written to expose, and it is why the arm was not chosen.

## 7. RULE APPLICATION VERSUS OUTCOME

- **Rule application: fully mechanical, no discretion.** Class membership is a function of
  closes at or before the qualifying bar; direction is a function of which edge; stop and
  target are the instructor's fixed numbers. **The assignment was frozen to disk before any
  outcome was computed** and is reproducible from `data/pt021_classes_arm{A,B}.csv`.
- **Days excluded: only for coverage, counted and labelled** (§1). The 118 / 106 days with no
  qualifying move are reported as their own category (Measure 3), not dropped.
- **Losers retained.** 186 / 168 SA stop-outs and 50 / 81 SW stop-outs are in §4, and the
  54 / 52 and 36 / 40 positions still open at 17:00 are marked to market.
- **The prohibited trade was actually priced**, in both classes, which is the whole design.
- **No parameter was adjusted.** The 25-pip qualifying floor, the 18/50 geometry, the seed and
  the iteration count are exactly as pre-registered.
- **No stop hunt was identified anywhere.** The discriminator is the geometric event only
  (§0), per `PT-021` §6.

## 8. WHAT THIS OBSERVATION DOES AND DOES NOT ESTABLISH

**Does:**
- **The SA / SW binary does not separate counter-trade outcomes.** Hit rates 0.222 vs 0.219
  (Arm A) and 0.204 vs 0.206 (Arm B); expectancy difference p = 0.404 / 0.679; no class
  clears its own N1 above the 85th percentile. **`INDISTINGUISHABLE FROM THE NULL`.**
- **This is a significant negative finding about V02's most confidently graded conditional**
  (`I27` `EXPLICIT`, Medium-High; `V02_SOURCE_NOTES.md` §6 *"a genuine binary test"*).
- The prohibited counter-trade is **−0.45 / −0.98 pips in SA and +2.15 / +0.10 pips in SW** —
  all four inside the unmodelled spread, none clearing break-even.
- **The classifier fires often**: SA on 51–57% of days, SW on 20–28%, both well over n ≥ 30,
  so no `PT-021` §5 re-issue is owed.
- **Class SW's membership grows 43% under a one-hour clock shift** (100 → 143).
- **The volatility confound the third arm was built for is largely absent** (p = 0.217 /
  0.931), and normalising changes nothing.
- Straightaway days continue marginally further than stop-hunt-first days in all four
  comparisons; **only one of the four reaches p < 0.05, and only on one arm.**

**Does not:**
- Identify a stop hunt, or bear on `A-002`, `A-049` or `C-006` in any way.
- Test the Market Maker Method entry rule (`A-007`, `A-011`, `A-039` all undefined).
- Establish that counter-trading *is* safe in class SA. It establishes that this sample cannot
  tell the classes apart, which is a different claim and a weaker one.
- Close `A-002`, `A-006`, `A-019`, `A-049`, `C-006` or any other record.
- Generalise beyond GBP/USD, 2014-01-05 → 2015-12-31, and the V02 printed box.
