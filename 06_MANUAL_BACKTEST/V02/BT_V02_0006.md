# BT_V02_0006 — PT-006: the NYC Reversal. Does the new session reverse the old one's direction?

> ## ⚠ MANDATORY SCOPE STATEMENT — `PT-006` §6, verbatim
>
> PT-006 tests whether session changeover is associated with a direction change on
> GBP/USD. It does **not** test *"a new market maker on duty"* — that is a claim about who
> is behind the price, which candle data cannot observe, and which this project treats as
> the instructor's teaching model rather than a microstructure fact
> (`V01_INTERPRETATION.md` §3 G7). It also does **not** test the take-profit rule, which
> requires an entry the course has not specified.

**Declared reading.** `PT-006` §3 writes *"close(09:00)"* without saying which bar. `open(t)`

08:45 inclusive — exactly the printed London session.

### 1a. A boundary-wrap bug, found and fixed before the sweep was read

The first implementation indexed a `session-day × 96-slot` matrix by **wrapped** slot. For
every changeover between **16:30 and 22:30** the post leg wrapped backwards across the 17:00
row boundary and was computed over the *preceding* 23 hours instead of the following hour —
**7 of the 24 sweep rows**, producing a spurious 0.707 at 16:30 that would have read as the
strongest reversal in the table.

Fixed by anchoring every leg to the changeover instant and stepping forward in **absolute
minutes**, which cannot wrap. **The 09:30 result is unchanged (0.4375): its window never
crossed the boundary.** The corrected 16:30 row is 0.5172 at n = 203. Recorded because a
number that survives into a report acquires authority a note never does (`D-030`'s reasoning).

## 3. RESULT — THE PRIMARY MEASURE. **THE OPPOSITE OF THE CLAIM ON ARM A**

| | **Arm A** (`UTC−5`) | **Arm B** (NY/DST) |
|---|---|---|
| **Reversal rate at 09:30** | **0.438 (112/256)** [0.378, 0.499] | **0.507 (112/221)** [0.441, 0.572] |
| Percentile within N2 | **2.7** | 44.3 |
| Rank among the 24 candidate changeover hours | **23 / 24** (descending) | 16 / 24 |
| Within-London control W2 / W1 | 0.476 / 0.486 | 0.518 / 0.541 |
| Flat legs (reported, not dropped) | 0 | — |

> ### **On Arm A the New York changeover is the second-*least* reversing hour of the 24 tested, and it sits at the 2.7th percentile of its own null.**
>
> A reversal rate of **0.438** means London's direction **continues** into the first New York
> hour on 56.2% of days. The claim predicts the opposite. This is not merely a failure to
> confirm — it is **below the null's 5th percentile in the continuation direction**, which is
> a directional finding in its own right.
>
> `PT-006` §5's row *"Indistinguishable from 50% → The claim fails at this sample. **Report
> prominently** — it would weaken the lesson's clearest actionable instruction, which is
> exactly the kind of result that must not be buried (`E25`)"* is the row that fires, and Arm A
> goes further than that row anticipated.
>
> **The mechanism the claim proposes is unavailable too.** `PT-006` §5's trap row —
> *"Elevated at 09:30 **and** within-session → the effect is generic mean reversion"* — cannot
> apply: the within-session controls are **below** 50%, and 09:30 is **below both of them**.

## 5. `D-031` — THE ARMS DIVERGE, AND THE SAMPLES ARE NOT THE SAME

| | Arm A | Arm B |
|---|---|---|
| n days | **256** | **222** |
| 09:30 reversal | **0.438** | **0.507** |
| Percentile within N2 | **2.7** | 44.3 |
| Rank of 09:30 among 24 | 23 | 16 |

**The divergence is confounded with the sample and must not be read as a pure clock effect.**
As in `BT_V02_0004` §5, requiring all 96 session slots removes **34 US-DST Mondays** from Arm B,
because the Arm-B week open shifts to 18:00 and leaves the 17:00–18:00 hour empty. **Arm B's
222 days are a seasonally-skewed subset of Arm A's 256** — winter-weighted at the week open.

So the honest statement is: **Arm A, on the fuller sample, contradicts the claim at percentile
2.7; Arm B, on a smaller and seasonally-skewed sample, returns nothing.** Neither supports the
claim, and **selecting either arm would be `E09`**. What the pair jointly establish is that
**no reading of the clock produces reversal at the New York changeover.**

## 7. WHAT THIS OBSERVATION DOES AND DOES NOT ESTABLISH

**Does:**
- **The NYC Reversal is not present on GBP/USD in W-A.** Arm A: **0.438 (112/256)**,
  percentile **2.7** of its own null, **rank 23 of 24** candidate changeover hours. Arm B:
  0.507, percentile 44.3.
- **On Arm A the effect runs the other way** — London's direction *continues* into the first
  New York hour on 56.2% of days, and continuation strengthens to 0.414 by the 17:00 close.
- **Ambient mean reversion is not the explanation and cannot be**: the within-London controls
  are 0.476 / 0.486, *below* 50%, and 09:30 is below both.
- **The two changeovers behave differently**: Asian→London reverses at 0.549 while
  London→New York continues at 0.438 — the instructor's named changeover is the one that
  does not reverse.
- **A boundary-wrap bug corrupted 7 of 24 sweep rows and was found and fixed before the sweep
  was read**; the 09:30 result was never affected.
- **Arm B's sample is a seasonally-skewed subset** (34 US-DST Mondays absent), so the arm
  divergence confounds clock with sample.

**Does not:**
- Test *"a new market maker on duty"* — unobservable in candle data (`PT-006` §6).
- Test the take-profit-at-changeover instruction, which needs an entry the course has not
  specified.
- Establish that London→New York continuation is *tradeable*. No trade is modelled here.
- Close `A-019`, `C-004` or any other record.
- Generalise beyond GBP/USD and 2015-01-04 → 2015-12-31.
