> open is 22:00 UTC**, not FXCM's 21:00 UTC.

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Claim under test | *"move away from you in a **unidirectional swing** for the rest of the week"* V02 `[00:14:37]`; and the instructor's own counter-case, *"Net change for the week zero"* V03 `[00:33:36]` |

| Window | **`W-C′` — 2013-01-06 → 2016-06-30**; 180 trading weeks |
| **Anchor rule, fixed in §3 and applied mechanically** | The week's high **or** low, **whichever the week's net direction runs away from**. Net up → anchor is the **low**; net down → anchor is the **high**. Not decided per week |
| Metric — efficiency | `\|close(week end) − price(anchor)\| ÷ Σ\|bar-to-bar movement\|` over post-anchor **15-minute** bars |
| Metric — monotonicity | Largest counter-directional retracement after the anchor, in pips and as a share |
| Metric — net-zero | Share of weeks whose close sits within **±25 pips** of the week open |
| Baselines | **arm 1** random anchor in the same week · **arm 2** anchor at another week's extreme *position* · **arm 3** N3 week-anchor shift. 1,000 draws each, seed `20260812` |

### §0b — the resolution is a first-class parameter, not a footnote

> **PATH RESOLUTION = 15-MINUTE BARS**, for every week and every control alike. **The M1
> corpus is NOT used for the path sum.**

Directional efficiency is `|net| ÷ path`, and the denominator **grows as the bar gets finer** —
the same week measured on M1 has a longer path than on M15, so efficiency is *lower* at finer
resolution. That is arithmetic, not a market property. **Every figure below is an M15 figure
and is not comparable with any efficiency computed elsewhere in this project on another
timeframe.** §5 row 5: *"Efficiency is high but the figure is quoted without its bar size →
**Not a result — a defect.**"*

## 2. THE THREE BASELINES, BUILT FIRST

| Baseline | What it holds fixed | Median efficiency | 5–95% |
|---|---|---|---|
| **arm 1** — random anchor, same week, equal-length span | week, volatility, span length | **0.0710** | [0.0656, 0.0766] |
| **arm 2** — anchor at another week's extreme *position* | week, span length, positional distribution | **0.0637** | [0.0576, 0.0694] |
| **arm 3** — N3 week-anchor shift | the price path | **0.1055** | [0.0984, 0.1139] |

> ### ⚠ What arm 2 does and does not control, stated rather than assumed
>
> `PT-029` §4 calls arm 2 the control for *"the mechanical fact that any span starting at a
> local extreme has elevated efficiency by construction"* and calls it *"the one that
> matters"*. Read literally — **and it is implemented literally** — the arm borrows the
> extreme's **timing**, not its **extremeness**: the anchor sits at a typical extreme
> *position* in this week, but need not be an extreme *of* it.
>
> **So arm 2 isolates POSITION, and arms 1 and 2 together BRACKET the extremeness effect
> rather than measuring it directly.** Building a control that anchored at a genuine "local
> extreme" would require a definition of *local extreme* that the course does not give, and
> **`D-030` forbids inventing one.** The limitation is recorded here rather than papered over,
> and it means **§5 row 2's outcome — *"exceeds arm 1 but not arm 2 → the claim is an artifact
> of anchoring at an extreme"* — cannot be cleanly distinguished by this design.** That is a
> property of the pre-registration, disclosed, not a failure of the run.

**Arm 3 (N3) is the strongest of the three at 0.1055**, and it is the one that does control for
extremeness — a sham week's extreme is a genuine extreme of that sham week.

## 3. RESULT

**Identical on both `D-031` arms.** Every metric here is a function of prices, the week's
realised open and close, and the extreme's location — **none of them references a clock time**,
so a timezone relabelling cannot move any of them. (Contrast `BT_V03_0003`, where the
midnight-anchored block arm does shift.)

| | Value |
|---|---|
| n | **180** |
| **median efficiency** | **0.1153** |
| bootstrap 95% CI on the median | **[0.1059, 0.1236]** |
| IQR | [0.0742, 0.1514] |
| 5–95% | [0.0395, 0.2729] |
| vs **arm 1** (0.0710) | **percentile 100.0** |
| vs **arm 2** (0.0637) | **percentile 100.0** |
| vs **arm 3 / N3** (0.1055) | **percentile 96.5** |

> ### **Post-extreme efficiency exceeds all three controls. `PT-029` §5 row 1 fires: "Genuine support for the unidirectional claim, beyond the extreme-anchoring artifact."**
>
> The margin over N3 — the control that *does* hold extremeness fixed — is narrow but real:
> 0.1153 against 0.1055, **percentile 96.5**, clearing the 95th.

### And now the magnitude, which points the other way

> ### **An efficiency of 0.1153 means the remainder of the week travels about 8.7 times its own net distance.**
>
> **Monotonicity** makes the same point without any ratio arithmetic:
>
> | | Median | IQR |
> |---|---|---|
> | largest counter-directional retracement, in pips | **81.4** | [59.3, 113.0] |
> | the same as a **share of the total move** | **0.451** | [0.291, 0.790] |
>
> **The median post-extreme run gives back 45% of its own net move in a single counter-move,
> and the upper quartile gives back 79%.** Whatever that is, the ordinary meaning of
> *"unidirectional"* is not it.
>
> **Both facts are true and both belong in the summary.** The post-extreme remainder is
> *more* directional than any control — and it is *not* directional in absolute terms. A
> report giving only the first would be `E24`; a report giving only the second would discard a
> result that clears three pre-registered nulls.

## 6. RULE APPLICATION VERSUS OUTCOME

PT-029 pre-registers **no trade and no decision point**. The **anchor rule was fixed in §3 and
applied mechanically**, never chosen per week. **All three controls were computed before the
observed efficiency distribution was read** (§7 step 5); the run order in
`data/pt029_report.txt` is the print order. §3a's dispositions were applied **by name**, with
the data hole removed **before** it could shorten a path. **The net-zero share is reported in
the headline, not the appendix** (step 6). **Every efficiency figure is reported with its bar
resolution** (step 7). Neither `PT-029` nor `PT-011` was edited.

**One limitation of the pre-registered design, disclosed in §2 rather than worked around:**
arm 2 as written controls for the extreme's *position*, not its *extremeness*, so §5 row 2's
outcome cannot be cleanly separated by this test. N3 (arm 3) is the control that does hold
extremeness fixed, and the result clears it by the narrowest of the three margins.

## 7. WHAT THIS OBSERVATION DOES AND DOES NOT ESTABLISH

**Does:**
- **Post-extreme directional efficiency exceeds all three pre-registered controls** — 0.1153
  against 0.0710, 0.0637 and 0.1055; percentiles 100.0, 100.0 and **96.5**. `PT-029` §5 row 1's
  comparison fires on both `D-031` arms.
- **And establishes that the effect is small in absolute terms.** The remainder of the week
  travels ~**8.7×** its net distance; the median run **retraces 45%** of its own net move, the
  upper quartile 79%. **The comparison supports the claim; the magnitude does not support the
  word *"unidirectional"*.**
- **V03's net-zero counter-case is rare: 8.9% (16/180)**, well below the *"say >25%"* the
  original file floated as a discussion aid — which §3a had already ruled was not a decision
  rule.
- Confirms **§0a.3's advance prediction** that the EU referendum week would sit near the top of
  the efficiency distribution (0.2496 against a 0.1153 median).
- Records that **arm 2 as pre-registered controls for position, not extremeness**, so §5 row 2
  is not cleanly separable by this design.

**Does not:**
- **Show that any of this is tradeable.** The anchor is knowable only after the week ends.
  `CLASS: DESCRIPTIVE`, and §6 forbids any other reading.
- Test the anchor point (`A-001`) or adopt any day count (`C-001` untouched).
- Support any efficiency figure quoted **without its bar size** — §0b makes the resolution a
  required parameter and every figure here is **M15**.
- Support any statement about what net-zero weeks are like — that subset is **16**, descriptive
  only.
- Generalise beyond GBP/USD and 2013-01-06 → 2016-06-30. **The October 2016 flash crash — the
  archetypal maximally-efficient post-extreme run — is in HOLDOUT and was not opened.**
