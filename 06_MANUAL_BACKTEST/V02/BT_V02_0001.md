# BT_V02_0001 — PT-018: the two-hour time stop. Is "not in profit yet" predictive?

> ## ⚠ MANDATORY SCOPE STATEMENT — `PT-018` §6, verbatim
>
> PT-018 tests a **management** rule on a **proxy entry**. The instructor's rule applies to
> his own entry (second-leg close, `A-007`; TDI, `A-039`), and the second-leg clock reset at
> `[00:34:51]` is **not modelled at all** because the term is undefined — so this test
> measures a *stricter* rule than the one taught, and the difference favours finding the
> time stop worse than it is. That direction of bias is stated here so the result can be
> read with it.

### 1a. Measure 3 is `DEFERRED`, not skipped — `D-030` / `D-019`

`PT-018` §3 Measure 3 is the second-leg clock reset — *"If you see a second leg, restart the
clock"* `[00:34:51]`. It is **NOT MODELLED**, by pre-registration, because **`A-007`
("second leg") is undefined across V01–V06** and `D-030` forbids substituting an
approximation.

Under `D-019` this is **`DEFERRED`, not `NOT APPLICABLE`**: the subject matter exists and the
work is performable in principle; it waits on the lesson that defines a second leg. It is
recorded here, as `PT-018` §3 requires it be recorded *"in every report of this test"*.

**Its direction of bias is known and is not neutral.** Modelling the reset would let some
stalled positions restart their clock and survive; omitting it closes them sooner. **This
test therefore measures a stricter rule than the one taught, and the bias runs toward finding
the time stop *worse* than it is.** Since the finding below is that the time stop is worth
approximately nothing, this bias works *against* the conclusion rather than for it — which is
the direction that makes a null result trustworthy.

## 2. RESULT — MEASURE 1. **EARLY PROGRESS IS STRONGLY PREDICTIVE. THIS PART IS SUPPORTED**

**Arm A**

| Class | n | target / stop / open at 17:00 | Eventual hit rate | Expectancy if LET RUN | pct vs N1 (hit / exp) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `PROGRESSED` | 32 | 20 / 3 / 9 | **0.870 (20/23)** [0.679, 0.955] ⚠ | **+36.93** [+28.90, +44.25] | 60.9 / 85.7 |
| `STALLED` | 150 | 22 / 53 / 75 | 0.293 (22/75) [0.202, 0.404] | +5.61 [+1.93, +9.57] | 81.3 / 90.2 |
| *Resolved before T+2h* | 195 | 24 / 171 / 0 | 0.123 (24/195) [0.084, 0.177] | −9.63 [−12.42, −6.14] | — |

**Arm B**

| Class | n | target / stop / open at 17:00 | Eventual hit rate | Expectancy if LET RUN | pct vs N1 (hit / exp) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `PROGRESSED` | 31 | 19 / 4 / 8 | **0.826 (19/23)** [0.629, 0.930] ⚠ | **+34.04** [+24.88, +42.03] | 48.1 / 65.2 |
| `STALLED` | 165 | 25 / 65 / 75 | 0.278 (25/90) [0.196, 0.378] | +5.06 [+1.40, +8.68] | 62.6 / 73.7 |
| *Resolved before T+2h* | 189 | 21 / 168 / 0 | 0.111 (21/189) [0.074, 0.164] | −10.44 [−13.32, −7.21] | — |

> ⚠ **`SAMPLE INSUFFICIENT FOR INFERENCE — descriptive only`** on the `PROGRESSED` hit rate.
> The class has 32 / 31 trades, clearing the floor, but only **23 / 23** resolved to stop or
> target; the rest were still open at 17:00. The 0.870 / 0.826 hit rates may not be quoted
> anywhere without this label in the same sentence.

**`PROGRESSED` − `STALLED` mean expectancy = +31.32 pips (Arm A) / +28.98 pips (Arm B),
label-shuffle permutation two-sided p = 0.000 on both arms** (1,000 shuffles, seed
`20260812`).

> ### **The claim underneath the rule holds, and it holds strongly.**
>
> A position that is +25 pips or better at two hours ends **~31 / ~29 pips per trade better**
> than one that is not, on the same entry, in the same window, with the same stop and target.
> `PT-018` §2's null hypothesis — *"Early progress carries no information about the eventual
> result"* — **is rejected on both arms at p = 0.000.**
>
> The gradient runs all the way down: `PROGRESSED` +36.93 / +34.04 → `STALLED` +5.61 / +5.06
> → *resolved-before-2h* −9.63 / −10.44. **The instructor's intuition that the clock tells you
> something is correct.**

**And the class positions are not distinguishable from the null.** `STALLED` sits at
percentile 81.3 / 62.6 on hit rate and 90.2 / 73.7 on expectancy of its own N1 — **nothing
clears 95.** The *separation* between the classes is real; the *level* of either class is
what matched random entry would produce. Both statements are in the data and both are here.

One further reading, from the N1 baseline: **the trigger population stalls *less* than random
entry does.** Share of live positions stalled at T+2h is **0.824 (150/182) / 0.842 (165/196)**
against an N1 null of **0.908 [0.872, 0.940] / 0.902 [0.864, 0.937]** — below the null's 5th
percentile on both arms. The 25–50 pip location trigger does move price along faster, which
is the same fact `BT_V04_0003` §4 found from the other direction.

## 3. RESULT — MEASURE 2. **THE RULE ITSELF IS WORTH NOTHING**

`PT-018` §7 step 4 requires this be reported **in pips of expectancy, never as a win-rate
delta alone**.

| | Arm A | Arm B |
|---|---|---|
| `STALLED` positions | 150 | 165 |
| **LET RUN** to stop / target / 17:00 | **+5.61 pips** [+1.93, +9.57] | **+5.06 pips** [+1.40, +8.68] |
| **CLOSED at T+2h** at the mark | **+6.13 pips** [+4.71, +7.52] | **+5.06 pips** [+3.55, +6.55] |
| **VALUE OF THE TIME STOP** | **+0.52 pips** per stalled trade | **−0.00 pips** per stalled trade |
| Percentile within N1 | **57.8** | **46.7** |
| Paired permutation two-sided p | **0.803** | **0.998** |
| Spread over **all** trades | **+0.203 pips/trade** | **−0.002 pips/trade** |
| *Win rate — reported second, never alone* | *0.527 → 0.733* | *0.497 → 0.661* |

> ### **`INDISTINGUISHABLE FROM THE NULL`. The time stop truncates at random.**
>
> Closing every stalled position at two hours is worth **+0.52 pips (Arm A)** and **−0.00 pips
> (Arm B)** per stalled trade. Against an N1 null whose own median value is +0.23 / +0.13
> pips, the observed values sit at **percentile 57.8 / 46.7** — the middle of the null.
> Permutation p = 0.803 / 0.998. **Spread across the whole trigger population the rule is
> worth +0.20 and −0.002 pips per trade, which is inside the spread this test declines to
> model.**
>
> `PT-018` §5 named this outcome in advance and named it *"the classic time-stop result, and
> the most likely one"*:
>
> > *"Closing `STALLED` positions **improves** the win rate but **reduces** total expectancy →
> > Report both figures; a win-rate improvement alone is the kind of number that gets quoted
> > out of context."*
>
> **That row fires almost exactly.** The win rate improves substantially — **0.527 → 0.733 on
> Arm A and 0.497 → 0.661 on Arm B** — while expectancy moves by half a pip and nothing. A
> practitioner reporting *"the time stop raised my win rate from 53% to 73%"* would be
> reporting a true number that means nothing about profitability. **The only difference from
> §5's prediction is that expectancy is not measurably *reduced* here either; it is flat.**
>
> **The rule described as *"not negotiable"* is, on this proxy entry in this window, a
> coin-flip dressed as discipline.** `E25` requires this be reported with equal prominence to
> §2's positive finding, and it is.

printed number is the instructor's and it survives the ranking; it survives it into a null.

| Cut | Arm A: stalled / progressed · `STALLED` hit · value | Arm B: stalled / progressed · `STALLED` hit · value |

## 5. THIRD BASELINE — EXPECTANCY PER HOUR OF EXPOSURE

Positions are open for different durations by construction, so per-trade expectancy is not
comparable across classes (`PT-018` §4). Resolved trades only.

| Class | Arm A: median hold · pips/hour | Arm B: median hold · pips/hour |
|---|---|---|
| `PROGRESSED` | 194 min · **+13.47** (n = 23) ⚠ | 195 min · **+12.39** (n = 23) ⚠ |
| `STALLED` | 223 min · **−0.46** (n = 75) | 206 min · **−1.01** (n = 90) |
| *Resolved before T+2h* | 34 min · **−69.82** (n = 183) | 32 min · **−57.88** (n = 180) |

⚠ `SAMPLE INSUFFICIENT` on the `PROGRESSED` rows (n = 23 resolved).

**On a per-hour basis a stalled position is earning approximately zero and a progressed one
is earning double digits.** This sharpens §2's finding rather than changing it: the *capital*
in a stalled trade is idle, not bleeding. Which is exactly why closing it gains nothing — a
time stop can only recover what the position was losing, and it was not losing.

The −69.82 / −57.88 pips/hour on trades resolved inside two hours is the 18-pip stop firing
fast, and it is the dominant cost in the whole population. **The trades that hurt are gone
long before the time stop could reach them.**

## 7. RULE APPLICATION VERSUS OUTCOME

- **Rule application: fully mechanical, no discretion.** Entry, direction, stop, target,
  classifier, cut and checkpoints are all fixed in advance. Nothing was judged, so nothing
  was graded.
- **Days excluded: only for coverage, counted and labelled** (§1b).
- **Losers retained.** 171 / 168 of the sub-two-hour stop-outs are the largest single block in
  the population and they are in every table above. 75 / 75 positions still open at 17:00 are
  marked to market rather than dropped.
- **Positions that could not be classified are counted, not swept.** The 6 / 8 trades whose
  session ended before T+2h are reported as their own category rather than folded into
  `STALLED`, which would have inflated it with days that simply triggered late.
- **No parameter was adjusted.** The +25 cut, the 18/50 geometry, the three checkpoints, the
  seed and the iteration count are exactly as pre-registered. The +30 and +40 cuts are run as
  pre-registered sensitivities, and **the primary remains +25 regardless of which performed
  best**.
- **Measure 3 not modelled**, per `D-030`, with its bias direction stated (§1a).

## 8. WHAT THIS OBSERVATION DOES AND DOES NOT ESTABLISH

**Does:**
- **Early progress is strongly predictive of eventual outcome.** `PROGRESSED` beats `STALLED`
  by **+31.32 / +28.98 pips per trade, p = 0.000 on both arms**. `PT-018` §2's null is
  rejected.
- **The time stop built on that fact is worth +0.52 / −0.00 pips per stalled trade**
  (percentile 57.8 / 46.7 of its own null; p = 0.803 / 0.998) — **`INDISTINGUISHABLE FROM THE
  NULL`**, and +0.203 / −0.002 pips spread over all trades.
- **It raises the win rate from 0.527 → 0.733 and 0.497 → 0.661 while doing so** — the exact
  number `PT-018` §5 warned would be quoted out of context.
- **The rule can only act on ~half the population**; 195/383 and 189/393 trades are already
  resolved (overwhelmingly stopped out) before two hours elapse.
- **T+2h ranks first of {1h, 2h, 3h} on both arms** — first among three values near zero.
- All three of the instructor's thresholds (25/30/40) give values between −0.09 and +0.52.
- A stalled position earns **−0.46 / −1.01 pips per hour** — idle, not bleeding, which is why
  closing it recovers nothing.

**Does not:**
- Test `CL1`, the entry rule, or anything requiring `A-007`, `A-011` or `A-039`.
- Model the second-leg clock reset (§1a) — **`DEFERRED` under `D-019`**, and the omission
  biases *against* the time stop, not for it.
- Establish that the time stop is harmful. It is not measurably anything.
- Close `A-007`, `A-019`, `C-001` or any other record.
- License adopting T+2h, +25 pips, or any other checkpoint into `12_MASTER_SPEC` /
  `13_MACHINE_SPEC` — `D-010` keeps a number found here out of the spec, and nothing here was
  found anyway.
- Generalise beyond GBP/USD, 2014-01-05 → 2015-12-31, and this proxy entry.
