# BT_V02_0007 — PT-007: the two named vector-candle clock windows, 8:31 and 4:30 London

> ## ⚠ MANDATORY SCOPE STATEMENT — `PT-007` §6, verbatim
>
> PT-007 tests whether two named clock minutes carry outsized GBP/USD range. It is **not**
> a test of the "vector candle" (`A-035`, undefined), **not** a test of the stop hunt
> (`A-002`/`A-049`, undefined and disputed between two guest accounts), and **not**
> evidence of dealer intent. It measures a clock and reports a clock.

> ## ⚠ WHAT THIS TEST CANNOT DO — `PT-007` §3a, stated before the result
>
> **No economic calendar exists in this corpus.** This test therefore cannot separate
> *"the dealer places a move at 8:31"* from *"US data is released at 8:30"*. Those are
> different explanations of the same observation, **and the second is the mundane one.**
> A report that read a clock result as evidence of dealer intent would be exactly the
> inference this project's `G7` refuses.

## 5. `D-031` — THE ARMS DIVERGE, WITH THE SAME MECHANISM AS `BT_V02_0005`

| | Arm A | Arm B |
|---|---|---|
| Profile rank of the 08:30 bar | **1 / 96** | 8 / 96 |
| Profile rank of the 04:30 bar | **2 / 96** | 6 / 96 |
| Busiest slots overall | **08:30, 04:30** | **09:30, 05:30** |
| Nulls cleared (of 6 metric-bar cells) | **6 of 6** | 1 of 6 |

**Arm B's peaks are Arm A's peaks displaced by exactly one hour** — 08:30→09:30, 04:30→05:30.
This is the same displacement `BT_V02_0005` §5 identified, from the same cause: Arm A is fixed
`UTC−5`, Arm B is New York local, and during US DST the two differ by an hour. **Arm A's 08:30
slot blends two events — the summer New York open and the winter data release — into one bin,
which is precisely why it is the single largest slot on that arm.**

> **Both arms agree that the named bars are top-decile features.** They disagree on whether
> those bars clear the pre-registered nulls, and **the disagreement is a clock artifact with
> an identified mechanism, not a measurement failure.** Neither arm is selected; `D-031`
> forbids it, and Arm B's smaller seasonally-skewed sample (§0) is a second reason not to.

> ### **The effect is emphatically not uniform across weekdays, and the pattern is a release calendar.**

> The **08:30 bar** more than doubles from Monday (11.60 pips) to Friday (27.10) and Thursday

> and Wednesday (23.47, 23.79) — **04:30 fixed `UTC−5` is 09:30 London in winter**, the UK
> data hour and the London equity open.
>
> **This is exactly the finding `PT-007` §3 pre-registered the weekday split to catch**, and
> it makes §3a's caveat concrete rather than formal. The honest report, in §3a's own words:
> *"outsized range is concentrated in the named bars; the cause is not identified and the
> obvious candidate is the release schedule itself."*
>
> **Monday — the weekday with almost no scheduled releases — is the weakest day for both bars
> on both arms.** A dealer-intent explanation has no reason to take Mondays off. A release
> calendar does.

## 8. WHAT THIS OBSERVATION DOES AND DOES NOT ESTABLISH

**Does:**
- **On Arm A the two minutes V02 names are the two busiest 15-minute slots of the GBP/USD day
  — ranks 1 and 2 of 96** — and clear **all three** pre-registered nulls (M1 pct 1.8 / 3.0;
  M2 97.2 / 98.3; M3 96.5 / 95.3).
- Both bars run at **more than 2× the day's median bar on 45–49% of days** and hold the day's
  extreme 3–4× more often than a uniform 1-of-96 bar would.
- **At M1 resolution the instructor is one minute off**: 04:30 is rank 1 of 1,440 and 08:30 is
  rank 2, while the named **08:31 is rank 4**. The move is at the release instant.
- **On Arm B the named bars remain top-decile (ranks 8 and 6) but clear only 1 of 6 null
  cells**, with the peaks displaced one hour to 09:30 and 05:30 — the same DST mechanism as
  `BT_V02_0005` §5.
- **The effect is strongly weekday-dependent** — 08:30 peaks Thu/Fri, 04:30 peaks Tue/Wed, and
  Monday is weakest for both. **This is the shape of a release calendar.**

**Does not:**
- Test the "vector candle" (`A-035`), the stop hunt (`A-002`/`A-049`), or **dealer intent**.
- **Separate dealer placement from the news schedule** — impossible without an economic
  calendar (`PT-007` §3a), and §6's weekday pattern indicates the mundane explanation.
- Support *"concealing them behind news and announcements"* as a mechanism. It confirms the
  **clock**, and the clock is equally consistent with the market simply reacting to data.
- Close `A-035`, `A-002`, `A-049`, `A-019`, `C-004` or `C-006`.
- Generalise beyond GBP/USD and 2014-01-05 → 2015-12-31.
