|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| **Sun (arm 2)** | 180 | **7.00** | 31.4 | 4.486 | 11.87 | 0.126 |

**The ranking, Sun+Mon among the five two-day spans** (denominator **5**, n = 180 weeks,
rank 1 = **lowest** range):

| Form | mean rank | rank-1 share | chance |
|---|---|---|---|
| **RAW range** | **1.65** | **0.633 (114/180)** [0.561, 0.700] | 3.00 / 0.200 |
| **NORMALISED — pips/hour (governs, §0c.1)** | **2.93** | **0.233 (42/180)** [0.178, 0.300] | 3.00 / 0.200 |
| √-time (added, §3) | 2.23 | 0.428 (77/180) [0.358, 0.501] | 3.00 / 0.200 |

Rank histogram, normalised: `r1=42  r2=35  r3=33  r4=33  r5=37` — **almost flat.**

> ### **`PT-031` §5 row 5, verbatim, is what happened: *"Sun+Mon wins on raw range but not on normalised range → The most likely trap in this test, and it is arithmetic. A 31-hour span containing less range than a 48-hour one is not a finding."***
>
> On raw range Sun+Mon is the quietest span in **63%** of weeks — an apparently decisive
> confirmation. **Normalised, it is indistinguishable from chance** (0.233 against 0.200, mean
> rank 2.93 against 3.00). **§0c.1 pre-registers the normalised form as governing, and it
> governs: `PT-031` §5 row 3 fires — "Sun+Mon ranks mid-pack → the analogy has no measurable
> support at this sample. Report prominently."**

### ⚠ But the pre-registered normalisation is not neutral either, and this must not be buried

**Range does not grow linearly with time.** For a random-walk path it grows roughly as **√T**.
So **both pre-registered forms are biased, in opposite directions**:

- **raw range** favours the **longer** span — the bias §0c.1 was written to catch;
- **pips per hour** *over-corrects* and favours the **shorter** span — a bias §0c.1 does not
  mention, and one that works **against** Sun+Mon in this ranking.

A √-time normalisation sits between them, and on it **Sun+Mon ranks first in 42.8% of weeks
against a 20% chance rate** (mean rank 2.23).

> **So the three forms give three answers: strongly supported (raw), not supported
> (pips/hour), moderately supported (√-time).** The pre-registration names pips/hour as
> governing and **this observation abides by that** — but **a verdict that flips with an
> arbitrary choice of normalisation is a weak verdict, and saying so is the honest report.**
> The √-time column is added **alongside** both pre-registered forms, not in place of either,
> and `D-030` forbids resolving the choice by preference now that the outcomes are visible.

**N3 (§4 arm 2)** points the same way as the governing form: Sun+Mon's normalised range sits at
**percentile 5.3** of the sham week's first-31-hours distribution on both arms — i.e. lower
than most shifted anchors, but **not clearing the 5th percentile.**

> ### **Metric 3 answers the second half of the claim, and the answer is no.**
>
> `PT-031` §5 row 2: *"Low range but no boundary relevance → Half the claim. **'Quiet'** and
> **'the level the dealer later exploits'** are different assertions and this outcome separates
> them — which is the most useful thing this test can do."*
>
> **The week's extreme forms beyond Sun+Mon's edge in 180 of 180 weeks — every single one — by
> a median of 134.3 pips**, the largest margin of any two-day span. §3c predicted the share
> would be near-certain by construction and directed that **the distance is the informative
> quantity**; the distance says the span's boundaries are **not** where the week later turns.
> The complementary subset (extreme *inside* the span) is **0**, so nothing can be said about
> it — the §4.1 label applies vacuously.
>
> **Metric 2 and Metric 4 both run against the claim too.** Sun+Mon contains only **31.7%** of
> the remaining week's bars — *lower* than Mon+Tue (0.388), Tue+Wed (0.698) and Wed+Thu
> (0.838) — and its edges are first breached after a median of **4.75 hours**, *sooner* than
> every control. **Both of those metrics are mechanically confounded** — a later span has less
> remaining week to contain and fewer hours in which to be breached — so they are reported and
> **not** treated as independent evidence. They do not rescue the claim; they do not need to.

## 7. WHAT THIS OBSERVATION DOES AND DOES NOT ESTABLISH

**Does:**
- **On the governing (length-normalised) metric, Sun+Mon ranks mid-pack** — mean rank 2.93 of
  5 against a chance 3.00, rank-1 share 0.233 against 0.200, on both `D-031` arms. **§5 row 3:
  the analogy has no measurable support at this sample.**
- **Demonstrates the arithmetic trap §5 row 5 predicted**: on *raw* range Sun+Mon is the
  quietest span in **63%** of weeks, and that is a 31-hour span being compared with 48-hour
  ones.
- **Establishes that the verdict depends on the normalisation, and that the pre-registered one
  is not neutral either** — raw favours long spans, pips/hour over-corrects and favours short
  ones, √-time sits between and gives 0.428. **A verdict that flips with the normalisation is
  a weak verdict.**
- **Separates the two halves of the claim, which §5 row 2 calls the most useful thing this test
  can do — and the second half fails outright.** The week's extreme forms beyond Sun+Mon's edge
  in **180 of 180** weeks, by a median of **134.3 pips**, the widest margin of any two-day span.
- **Reports all three of the instructor's own day-sets and adopts none.** His widest relaxation
  (Fri+Sun+Mon) is the quietest; **Sunday alone is the noisiest span of the entire week** at
  4.486 pips/hour.
- Confirms §0c.2 exactly: Arm B's DST weeks give Sunday 6 h and Sun+Mon 30 h.

**Does not:**
- **Define or measure "accumulation"** — `V02_SOURCE_NOTES.md` §3's block is **inherited
  unchanged** and nothing here closes it. This measures a named proxy.
- Treat Metrics 2 and 4 as independent evidence — both are mechanically confounded by how much
  week remains after the span.
- Adopt any of the three day-sets as doctrine, or resolve which normalisation is correct.
- Say anything about contract accumulation, which no candle chart can observe.
- Generalise beyond GBP/USD and 2013-01-06 → 2016-06-30. **The 2017 regime and the October 2016
  flash crash are in HOLDOUT and were not opened.**
