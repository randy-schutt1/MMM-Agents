**n = 41 included days**, above the floor.

| Arm | median `D` | `f(D>0)` | 95% CI | pct within N-P | pct within N2 | Arm reading |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A** (`UTC−5`) | **+3.4 p** | 0.610 (25/41) | [0.457, 0.743] | **89.6** | 54.0 | Leans toward the claim, clears **neither** null's 95th percentile |
| **B** (`UTC−4`) | **−4.5 p** | 0.390 (16/41) | [0.257, 0.543] | **5.8** | 37.8 | Leans **against** the claim; median difference has the **opposite sign** |

**Duration-normalised `O1b` — and this is where the arms agree:**

| Arm | median `Dn` (pips per hour) | `f(Dn>0)` |
|---|---|---|
| A | **+1.66** | 0.683 |
| B | **+1.16** | 0.585 |

Window medians: London **34.5 p** (A) / **35.5 p** (B); New York **35.7 p** (A) / **37.8 p** (B).

`O2`, descriptive: NY range ≥30 pips on **0.610** (A) / **0.756** (B); ≥50 pips on **0.195** (A)
/ **0.366** (B). The guest's *"anywhere from 30 to 50 pips"* band therefore describes the
**median** New York session in this period reasonably well, on both arms.

### 3a. The overall verdict, applying the pre-registered rule exactly as written

`PT-022` §6 requires its conditions to hold **in both arms**:

- **CONFIRMED** needs median `D` > 0 **and** `f` above the 95th percentile of **both** nulls in
  **both** arms. **Not met** — Arm B's median is negative, and neither arm clears N2.
- **CONTRADICTED** needs median `D` < 0 **or** `f` below the 5th percentile of both nulls, in
  **both** arms. **Not met** — Arm A's median is positive.

> ### **OVERALL VERDICT: `INDISTINGUISHABLE FROM THE NULL`.**
>
> The runner prints a per-arm label and calls Arm B `CONTRADICTED AS TAUGHT`. **That per-arm
> label is not the test's verdict** — the pre-registered rule is conjunctive across arms, and
> reporting Arm B's label as the result would be selecting the arm that produced the sharper
> answer, which is `E09` + `E24` and is exactly what `D-031` forbids.

### 3b. The arm divergence is the most interesting thing here, and it was predicted

**A one-hour shift of both windows flips the sign of the raw difference.** Arm A puts London
ahead by 3.4 pips; Arm B puts New York ahead by 4.5 pips. Same price path, same window lengths,
same days — only the clock moved.

`D-031` says divergence is a **finding**. What it finds: **for this claim, on this instrument, in
this period, the session clock is load-bearing.** An hour of ambiguity in where "London" starts
is enough to reverse which session looks bigger. **`A-019` — the course's own unresolved
timezone question — is therefore not a bookkeeping detail for claims of this shape.**

**This is not evidence about `A-019` itself** and may not be cited as such: V06 states no clock
at all, and the windows here are V02's. It is evidence that the *stakes* of `A-019` are real.

### 3c. What survives both arms, and what the guest may have been right about

**Per hour, London is the more active window in both arms** (`Dn` > 0, median +1.66 and +1.16
pips/hour; `f(Dn>0)` = 0.683 and 0.585). What does **not** survive is the raw comparison — and
`PT-022` §2a predicted precisely this asymmetry before any data was seen:

> *"The New York window is 36% longer, so the arithmetic is biased towards New York having the
> bigger range — which is the opposite of what the guest claims."*

So the honest reading, offered as a reading and not as a verdict:

> **The guest's claim looks true about intensity and unproven about total range.** London packs
> more movement into each hour; New York has more hours, and in one arm that is enough to make
> its absolute range larger. A trader hearing *"they don't usually run like London"* and
> concluding *"the New York session's range is smaller"* would be **taking a conclusion the data
> does not support**, even though the sentiment behind it survives.

**`O1` and `O1b` disagree, and `PT-022` §6 requires both be reported with neither preferred.**
That is done here: the disagreement *is* the result.
